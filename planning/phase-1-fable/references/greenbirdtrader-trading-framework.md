## 0b. The one-sentence model

He trades **failed breakouts and failed breakdowns of a session or hour range**.

He does **not** buy because price is cheap or sell because price is expensive.  
He buys because a breakdown **failed**. He sells because a breakout **failed**.

He calls this “playing ping pong with the market maker.” The edges of the table are session highs and lows. He waits for price to poke one edge, fail, and come back inside — then he aims at the opposite edge.

> “I didn’t short because price was high. I shorted because the breakout FAILED.”  
> “I’m not buying because price dropped. I’m buying because the breakdown FAILED.”  
> “This is my primary model. I use it for all the sessions highs and lows.”  
> “ONE MODEL FOR LIFE.”

## 3. The primary playbook: failed breakout / failed breakdown

This is the model he repeats until it is almost a template.

### 3.1 Build the box (do this first — no trade yet)

1. Pick the range you will use:
   - **NYAM default:** 9:00–10:00 AM ET.
   - **Asia default:** Asia session high and Asia session low.
   - **Portable version:** previous hour high / previous hour low (he has said “previous-hour highs get swept”).
   - **Daily context lines:** PDH, PDL, midnight open / TDO.
2. **Wait until that window is closed.**  
   For NYAM: “I wait until after 10 AM. The 9 to 10 AM range is established. No guessing beforehand.”
3. Draw:
   - Range high
   - Range low
   - Any bonus levels that sit near those edges (PDH, PDL, midnight open)

You now have a box. You do nothing inside the box until one edge is attacked.

### 3.2 The short (failed breakout)

1. Price **sweeps the range high** (and ideally also PDH if it is nearby).
2. Breakout traders get what they wanted — a new high.
3. Price **cannot hold above** the high.
4. Price **falls back inside** the range.
5. Confirmation he has named: a **close back below** the level — on at least one example, a **5-minute close below TDO** after the Asia-high sweep. On a PDL-sweep short: “The key for me is getting a **5 min close below**, once I got it I waited a few points and entered short.”
6. **Enter short** after that failure, not on the first tick above the high.
7. **Stop:** above the sweep wick / above the invalidation (he has said “stop right above PDL” when PDL was the swept level).
8. **First target:** the **low of the same range**.
9. After first target: take partials, **move stop to breakeven**, leave runners toward lower objectives (session low, measured move, next liquidity).

### 3.3 The long (failed breakdown)

Exact mirror.

1. Wait for the range to exist (after 10 AM for NYAM).
2. Price **sweeps the range lows**.
3. Price **fails to stay below** them.
4. Price **reclaims the range**.
5. Extra confluence example he posted: the sweep also took **previous day’s low**.
6. **Enter long** on the failed breakdown, not because “it dipped.”
7. **First target:** range highs.
8. Partials, stop to breakeven, runners.

### 3.4 What is *not* a signal

- A sweep **alone**. “A sweep alone isn’t my signal. I’m watching what happens AFTER it. Does price hold above the Asia highs, or fail back inside?”
- “Price is high” or “price is low.”
- Predicting the next direction of NQ.
- Indicators, Discord calls, SMT, retail order flow.

### 3.5 Grading the setup

He scores setups. Reconstructed grading from his own words:

| Grade | What he has actually required |
|---|---|
| **A+** | Sweep of the relevant range **plus** failure back inside. Often also HTF bias, a 5-minute close beyond a key line (range / TDO / PDL), and a tight invalidation. He has called the stacked version “structure + liquidity + retracement + failure.” |
| **Bonus confluence (raises the grade)** | Simultaneous sweep of **PDH or PDL**; close beyond **midnight open / TDO**; golden pocket location; multi-timeframe agreement; already-bearish or already-bullish day. |
| **B+** | Directional idea **without** a range sweep. He said B+ is not easy to trade. On those days he shrinks size and takes 20–30 point scalps instead of hunting 100+ point A+ runners. |
| **No trade** | Range not finished. Sweep but price **holds** outside (true breakout). No close back inside. No clear invalidation. |

## 4. Second model: Golden Pocket continuation / reaction

Same philosophy (location → wait for failure → execute), different measuring stick.

### How he defines the zone

> “Measure the NYAM move from high to low, mark off the 50% and there is your discount zone. I use the **50%–61.8% fib retracement** as my golden pocket zone, pretty common.”

So:

1. Identify a completed impulse (e.g. NYAM high-to-low on a bearish day, or a larger HTF swing).
2. Draw Fibonacci from the start of that impulse to the end.
3. The **50–61.8%** retracement is the pocket.
4. On a **bearish** day that pocket is a **short location** (retracement into premium/mid).
5. On a **bullish** day it is a **long location** (retracement into discount).

### What he waits for *in* the pocket

Not a blind limit at 61.8%.

From July 30 posts:

- Higher-timeframe bias first (“higher time frame bearish”).
- Price retraces ~50% into the golden pocket.
- **Clean rejection** / failure to continue through the zone.
- Then the trade.
- Invalidation: **above the zone** for a short.
- Once in profit: stops to breakeven, partials along the way.

He summarized the whole thing as four words:

**Bias. Location. Confirmation. Execution.**

A fully stacked short he called “picture-perfect Green Bird”:

1. Bearish day  
2. Retracement ~50% into the golden pocket  
3. Sweep above **PDL**  
4. Failure  
5. Short, stop just above PDL, target the lows  

That is the range-failure model **plus** the fib location.

### Premium / discount reminder

- Longs: he wants the pullback in **discount** (at/below 50% of the bullish range).
- Shorts: he wants the bounce in **premium** (at/above 50% of the bearish range).
- Golden pocket sits on that 50–61.8 band.

## 7. Entry, stop, target, management — the mechanical rules

Pulled only from things he has written or shown on tickets.

### Entry

- After **failure + reclaim**, not during the sweep.
- Preferred confirmation: **5-minute close** back inside / back beyond TDO or PDL.
- Then a small wait (“waited a few points”) rather than chasing the close tick.
- He strikes when the A+ picture is there. “When you see your setup, you gotta act. No hesitation.”
- He does **not** need the market to make a brand-new high for a short if the failure already happened at the level (he challenged a comment with “How? It never made a new high?” — meaning the *hold* above matters more than a literal new high label).

### Stop / invalidation

- Beyond the **sweep extreme** or beyond the **zone**.
- Examples from posts: stop right above PDL on a PDL-sweep short; “clearly defined invalidation above the zone” on a golden-pocket short.
- Screenshot tickets (illustrative, not a fixed formula):
  - Long after NYAM low sweep: stop ~32.5 points below, first targets stacked above, R:R shown ~6.7.
  - Short after 9–10 high sweep: stop ~34.5 points above, targets down toward PDL, R:R shown ~8.7.
  - Golden-pocket / PDL short: stop ~49.75 points above PDL, R:R shown ~4.2.
- After first objective pays: **stop to breakeven**. “Risk removed.” “From that point on I had already guaranteed myself a green trade no matter what happened next.”

### Targets

- **First objective = opposite side of the range you used.**
  - Short the 9–10 high failure → first target is the **9–10 low**.
  - Long the 9–10 low failure → first target is the **9–10 high**.
  - Asia high failure → target **Asia low**.
- Then runners toward next liquidity (session low/high, PDL, measured extension).
- **Partials every 25 points** (stated on the Sep 8 NYAM long).
- He will take partials *instead of* waiting for the entire theoretical move. “I took partials rather than waiting for the entire move.”
- Limit orders on screenshots are often parked in a **ladder** (~25–40 point spacing), matching the 25-point partial rule.

### Position sizing and “pressing”

- **A+ day:** full conviction, can look for 100–200 point swings, runners on.
- **No A+ / messy day:** stay small, 20–30 point scalps, no reason to size up. “Green is green.”
- **Winners:** he learned to **add to winners** (average **up**, not down). “I don’t average down. I average up.”
- **After the plan is paid:** flatten / lock accounts. Kill switch in Tradesyncer. “There comes a point where enough is enough.”
- Combined-fleet P&L numbers are marketing-noisy. For *rules*, ignore the dollar figure; keep the structure.

### What a typical ticket looks like on his screenshots

- Session box shaded (green/grey “Green Bird” watermark).
- Sweep wick marked through the box edge, often with a small “NY” tag at the session boundary.
- Red stop box beyond the wick.
- Green target box toward the other side of the range / next level.
- Multiple limit exits in a column.
- Open P&L, R:R, and contract quantity on the position bubble.

## 8. Session-by-session recipes

### A. NYAM (his flagship)

```
After 10:00 AM ET
9:00–10:00 range is complete
IF price sweeps 9–10 high
   AND fails / closes back inside
   AND (bonus) also swept PDH
THEN short
   stop = above sweep
   T1 = 9–10 low
   partials every ~25 pts
   stop → breakeven
   runners lower

IF price sweeps 9–10 low
   AND fails to stay below / reclaims range
   AND (bonus) also swept PDL
THEN long
   stop = below sweep
   T1 = 9–10 high
   same management
```

He has printed this as a numbered checklist (Aug 28):

1. Wait for 9:00–10:00 to close.  
2. Wait for a sweep of the 9–10 highs (bonus: PDH).  
3. Watch the breakout fail — clean rejection, back into the range.  
4. Strike the short. First objective = low of 9–10. Then runners, stop to BE.

### B. Asia / midnight

```
Mark Asia high, Asia low, midnight open
IF Asia high is swept
   AND price closes back below (ideally also below midnight open / TDO on the 5m)
THEN short toward Asia low

IF Asia low is swept
   AND failure back inside
THEN long toward Asia high
```

Same ping-pong sentence: “Asia highs and lows are the edges of the table. I wait for price to sweep one side, fail back inside, then trade the reversal toward the opposite edge.”

### C. Previous-hour version

Identical logic on the **last completed 60-minute box**. He has taken two NYAM shorts in one morning this way: first hour high swept and failed (~50 pts), then the market “served it up again” (~100 pts).

### D. Trend day / news day overlay

If overnight already did the clean reclaim (e.g. PDL swept and held):

- Bias is set.
