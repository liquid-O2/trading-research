**Phase 1.5 search and reconstruction**

The first pass samples different mechanisms in the way the user's music example samples genres. It should not consume its budget testing dozens of almost identical clock shifts. An example first screen for a formation problem could compare a clock-defined reference, an activity-defined reference, and a confirmed-balance reference. **One shared forward-volatility model in Phase 2**

The user confirmed **Garman–Klass, Yang–Zhang, HAR-RV information and implied-volatility features fitted together in one model**. Historical estimators provide measurements; the joint model learns how their values and histories predict future volatility. HAR-RV supplies a useful multi-horizon realized-volatility structure; its original forecasting framework is described in [Corsi's paper](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article-abstract/7/2/174/856522).

The deliverable is one independently testable volatility expert with a shared output interface. Separate historical estimators and standalone forecasts are comparison baselines, not a requirement to make each input its own fitted expert. A regularized model extending HAR with the other inputs is a sensible initial joint model; a nonlinear challenger can test interactions using the same feature contract. The model family remains a measured choice.

The feature inventory includes all major requested dimensions, staged through feature-group comparisons:

| Input group | Candidate information |
| --- | --- |
| Historical and realized variation | Garman–Klass, Yang–Zhang and other justified range estimators; realized variance at several scales; HAR-style daily/weekly/monthly histories; upside/downside variation; jumps; overnight/session differences; volatility persistence. |
| IV level and expected movement | ATM IV, strike- and delta/moneyness-indexed IV, option-implied expected-move measures, and a sufficiently covered strip of option prices for broader implied-variance measures. |
| Skew, smile and tails | Put/call wing slopes, risk reversals, butterflies/curvature, tail steepness and asymmetry, and changes in those measurements. Keep strike/delta and expiry coordinates explicit. |
| Term and event structure | Expiry-specific IV, forward variance, near/far slopes and curvature, same-day expiry effects, known event-related variance and maturity composition. |
| Surface dynamics | Intraday changes in IV/skew/term structure, volatility of those changes, and differences between implied and recent realized variation. |
| Cross-market volatility | Comparable features from NDX/NDXP, SPX/SPXW, QQQ, SPY and NQ/ES options; relevant owned volatility-index/futures observations; time-varying agreements and differences. |
| Exposure, activity and market state | Explicit gamma, vega, vanna and justified additional exposure scenarios; prior OI, rolling volume, options flow, liquidity, time of day, auction state and scheduled-event information available at issue time. |

Do not turn a 30-day implied-volatility reading into a claimed forecast for 06:00–09:00 or 09:00–10:00 by an untested scaling rule. For example, Cboe defines VIX as SPX-option-implied expected volatility over 30 days; the project's shorter-window forecasts require their own calibration. [Cboe VIX FAQ](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/vix/faqs).

The main prediction contract records instrument, issue time, target interval, model version, available input vintages, missingness and uncertainty. Forecast future realized variance/volatility for the relevant horizons, with a separately scored range/excursion output or downstream conversion for the session consumers. Volatility, high–low range, directional excursion and reversal probability are different targets. An expected-range output is not automatically a P-zone or an exact reconstruction of EV.

**Intraday forecasting is a required Phase 2 capability.** The same jointly fitted volatility expert must support forecasts issued during the session using information observed so far. Its target intervals include the next declared intraday window, the remaining part of the current session, later sessions in the same account-day, and the remaining account-day. For example, a forecast issued at 09:45 may cover 09:45–10:00 and the remaining morning; these illustrate the interface rather than fix the research horizons. Full-day or pre-open forecasts alone do not complete this deliverable.

Refresh those predictions from observed intraday realized variation, the current IV surface, options activity/exposure estimates and relevant market state. Score each horizon's predicted distribution and uncertainty, separately from the companion expected-range/excursion output. Consumers include remaining movement/target ambition, expected compression or expansion, the suitability of each method's tactics, and later location/entry models. Changing the forecast as new inputs arrive is required; learning new model parameters inside the session remains a separately evaluated adaptation mechanism.

Evaluate the joint model against seasonal/rolling baselines, HAR alone, historical-only inputs and IV-only inputs, then remove feature groups to measure what each contributes. Require proper forecast loss, interval calibration and stability, plus improvement for downstream consumers where claimed. Combining inputs is the research hypothesis; correlated or noisy inputs can fail to help. Keep forecasts of remaining movement distinct from simply subtracting the distance already traveled from a daily range estimate.

**What makes a location or response better**

The user's criterion is substantial favorable movement with little adverse movement. The scoring must preserve the order of that movement. A location that eventually produces a large MFE after first crossing an unacceptable loss boundary is not the desired opportunity.

For each causally issued candidate, record side, location origin, response origin when present, NQ observation/entry reference, horizon, expiry, and the whole eligible path. Measure:

- The probability of reaching a specified favorable NQ distance before a specified adverse distance within the evaluation horizon.
- Adverse excursion before reaching the favorable objective, full-window MAE/MFE separately, time to each boundary, and unresolved outcomes.
- Favorable-move size, downside tails, probability calibration, opportunity counts, daily coverage and the effect of waiting for confirmation.
- Performance for both continuation and reversal; neither category receives automatic preference.

Compare a modest set of favorable/adverse thresholds and horizons selected on development data. This describes the trade-offs before committing to an exit optimizer. A single MFE/MAE ratio is insufficient, especially near zero MAE or when the extremes occur in the wrong order.

Phase 3 scores issued locations before adding new response learning, using frozen interaction and response benchmarks. Phase 4 measures the incremental information in the response and the executable entry delay. Pre-arrival levels and approach-refined versions must have separate records. Related-market triggers use the time at which the complete required evidence is available, not an earlier apparent turning point.

Also control for level count/density, zone width, distance and issue time. Generating more or wider levels must not receive credit simply because one overlaps a later move. Count all issued candidates, failed responses, non-interactions and unavailable populations in their correct denominators.

**Evaluation before an exit model, and later management**

Useful measurement does not require a learned exit model. Use two linked comparisons from 1.5 onward:

1. Ordered price/opportunity outcomes as above, retaining the whole path and multiple declared horizons.
2. A small fixed, causal execution/management benchmark with explicit entry timing, structural or predeclared invalidation, objective/expiry, account-day flattening and costs. Keep it unchanged when attributing a signal change.

This benchmark measures whether the observed advantage can survive a plausible way of taking and closing a trade. MFE is not booked profit, and hindsight-perfect exits are not an executable strategy. Where objects have different non-trading targets, first judge their own correctness/forecast calibration, then test their contribution through an unchanged consumer before claiming a trading improvement.

The recommended learned-response stage is 4: separate specialists for mechanisms such as failed-break/reclaim, accepted breakout/retest and absorption/reward, using the relevant native price/flow sequences. Compare rules, sequence-summary models and richer sequence models on the same available opportunities. NQ confirmation is an optional comparison for the custom cross-market family, not a mandatory gate.

Learned exit and management research follows the initial combined entry system. Start with identical entry populations so the contribution of an exit change is identifiable. Fresh re-entry is a new causal decision after new evidence. Partials require revisiting the one-contract sizing convention and are not included by default.

**Economic objective and acceptance**

Recommended research objective, using the user's permission to choose the approach: optimize net profit and downside jointly, retaining the best observed trade-offs instead of choosing a model from a single arbitrary weighted score. Treat $3,000 per trading day as the ambition, $1,000 per day as the user's minimum acceptance threshold, and a $1,000 loss from the day's starting balance as the confirmed daily risk limit. This avoids optimizing around a rigid $3,000 quota while preserving the later stated minimum.

The daily floor applies to the complete system under declared execution and management, not to every source strategy, forecast, location or isolated research component. A component can pass its own research test while the complete system still fails the user's economic acceptance requirement.

Report net P&L for every eligible account-day, fraction/count of days at or above $1,000 and $3,000, all shortfalls, zero-trade days, losses, median/worst day, and the full distribution. Do not replace this with average profit per traded day or remove days because no opportunity was selected. Under a strict daily floor, a day below $1,000 remains a requirement miss even if the overall mean is high.

Report the loss from day-start separately from intraday peak-to-trough and whole-test peak-to-trough drawdown. The latter remain useful downside diagnostics, but they are not the user's chosen $1,000 limit. The risk replay should monitor marked-to-market losses, model stopping/liquidation causally, and report execution overshoot; it must not clip simulated losses at exactly $1,000.

The final comparison must reflect the actual one-account/one-NQ sequence of trades, costs, concurrent-candidate conflicts and days with no trades. It cannot sum the best overlapping opportunities or add up MFE. Historical attainment describes the tested sample; it cannot guarantee a profit floor on future days. If the required daily floor is not supported, report that the system does not meet it.
