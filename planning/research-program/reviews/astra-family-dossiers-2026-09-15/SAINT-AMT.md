## 1. THE AUTHOR'S METHOD AS STATED

- S1 R/D — Start with accepted value and a higher-timeframe balance that fits traded structure; redraw an ill-fitting balance BEFORE trading. Value is an area, POC its busiest price, not its geometric midpoint; the roughly 68% value-area illustration does not make VAH/VAL the session’s actual extremes. RTVP pp.3–4; AMTL pp.3–4; TRAP p.3.
- S2 R/D — Classify profile structure. A balanced D profile gives useful extremes. A double distribution has two shelves connected by a low-volume bridge. A trending profile with no stable balance is left alone until balance forms. P or b begins with a drive, establishes a balance at the head/base, then offers a continuation on its break/retest. Saint prefers continuations and takes reversals relatively infrequently; no measured win rate or numerical classifier is supplied. RTVP pp.6–11.
- S3 R/D — At the selected area read acceptance/rejection, arrival speed and initiative, executed delta and price response. Fast purposeful arrival differs from a weak grind. An extreme is not an automatic reversal; a controlling side can continue without a retest as an observation, while a trade entry may still require confirmation. Large positive delta with no upward progress can support trapped-buyers inference, not identify actual trapped account owners. AMTL pp.5–7; WIC pp.3–6; TRAP pp.4–5.
- S4 R/D — Wait through two-sided “free game.” Let lower-timeframe control agree with the HTF read: a balance break, actual retest and follow-through demonstrate control. The 15-minute chart helps when the small chart is unclear; second retests are preferred in WIC p.10, not a universal fixed count for every route. If a break below is reclaimed/defended by buyers, revise the short interpretation. WIC pp.5,7–11.
- S5 E/R — TRAP’s short thesis combines an upper HTF balance extreme, heavy buying that fails, and separate prior AM and PM failures. Current price need not retag the exact daily extreme: an intraday balance near it can break down and retest. If the retest is missed, wait for another relevant test/daily extreme rather than chase. The two prior sessions are part of this example, not a universal rule for all Saint trades. TRAP pp.3–7.
- S6 R/E — At the retest confirm repeated aggressive selling INSIDE candle bodies, with DOM/footprint agreement, then take the planned short. Opposite initiative/reclaimed structure invalidates the directional read. No universal volume threshold, footprint ratio, two-bar count or precise stop-padding formula is printed. TRAP pp.7–9; WIC pp.8–10.
- S7 R/D — Destinations depend on the route: POC/fair value, the other shelf, the far edge, or return to the original balance. Repeated failures to get through POC favor chop/return; aggressive passage and held retest support travel to the far side. AMTL’s rough 80/20 claims lack a defined cohort and are not fixed transition probabilities. RTVP pp.5–8; AMTL pp.8–11.
- S8 R/E — Failed auction: price explores lower value, fails to accept and returns into the original balance, then read POC and subsequent acceptance. The example can penetrate deeply and take time; it does not require Sires’s narrow instant rejection at a separate older POC. AMTL pp.8–11. AMTL p.7 explicitly allows LVN rejection immediately OR rapid passage; the short checklist’s simpler “slice” language is not the whole rule.
- S9 E/R/D — In TRAP the target stays conservatively inside a realistic Asia range; author says a normal Asia session can move 150–160 points, uses normal size and keeps the target fixed instead of trailing it. These are case management/expectation, not an every-day range or universal 150-point take profit. Exact account size/quantity and general risk formula are unpublished. TRAP pp.8–10,12.
