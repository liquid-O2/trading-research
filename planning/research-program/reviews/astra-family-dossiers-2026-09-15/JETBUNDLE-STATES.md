## 1. THE AUTHOR'S METHOD AS STATED

- J1 R — The guide separates Jetbundle’s participation/state framework (pp.3–11) from Sires’s NQ application (pp.12–14). Read an auction as a process of providing, withdrawing and consuming liquidity, using submissions, cancellations and executions; reconcile DOM/tape/footprint impressions with the event record. When they disagree, trust the record. MATH pp.3–6.
- J2 R — Reject seven shortcuts: high volume=acceptance; aggressive buying=next tick up; resting size=support/resistance; large traded size=absorption; static imbalance=direction; value=one price; setup=fixed win rate. Measure participation and response. These are cautions about conditioning, not proofs that no empirical win-rate estimate can be defined. MATH pp.4,11.
- J3 R/D — Ask whether resting size refills as consumed or vanishes; compare aggression with price-displacement efficiency. Efficient displacement is discovery: do not fade it. Effort without displacement is only candidate absorption; require high aggression, low response AND opposite liquidity holding/refilling. Replenishment failure and a giving-way level are exhaustion. Mere traded size or a static screenshot is insufficient. MATH pp.6–8.
- J4 R/D — Local value is a regime of two-sided executions, frequent recent revisits and low aggression on both sides; moving VAH/VAL are not permanently fixed value. Five heuristic labels: B balance; A absorption; D discovery; E exhaustion; W withdrawal/cancels dominate. They discipline judgment, not an automatic entry/stop/target algorithm. Numerical definitions are unpublished. MATH pp.9–10.
- J5 R/E — Observe next-state probabilities conditional on current liquidity and pace. Diagonal is persistence; off-diagonal describes transitions, not trade P&L or an unconditional market edge. Illustration uses AAPL LOBSTER ten-level order-book data; neither AAPL nor ten levels is stated as a universal applicability restriction. No trading clock, position size, stop, target or management algorithm is supplied. MATH pp.3,10–11,16.
- J6 E, p.10 figure — Slide13/19 says “Heuristic on AAPL quantiles”; exact quantiles are not printed. Main guide says20,000 events, plotted interval is5,000–19,999 (15,000 displayed), not the entire sample. B/A look dense; D/W also occur; E has no visible marks in this displayed slice. Do not infer exact state counts or absence in all20,000 from pixel density.
- J7 E, pp.1/11 matrix — Slide14/19 rows→columns B,A,D,E,W: B=(.98,.00,.00,blank,.01); A=(.01,.94,.04,.00,.01); D=(.03,.12,.84,.00,.01); E=(blank,.19,blank,.81,blank); W=(.04,.00,.00,blank,.95). Blanks are unprinted, not assumed zero; rounded rows may not sum1. The text’s D→D84% and D→A12% match. Cover is a repeated matrix, not independent data.
- J8 R/D, Sires application only — Trade the current auction for intraday setups usually10 minutes–2 hours; inside a balance, wait for rejection or break before selecting direction. Low-volume nodes, shelves and ledges need a second transition into balance, otherwise a taper is only a tail. Shelf tapers gradually; ledge cuts off abruptly. Establish HTF cycle/location before LTF DOM/footprint/heatmap trigger; in balance consider extremes, in discovery do not fade an efficient trend. MATH pp.12–15. “90% DOM noise” is an unquantified teaching assertion.
