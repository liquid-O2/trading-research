## 1. THE AUTHOR'S METHOD AS STATED

**R = stated instruction; E = example only; D = discretionary selection.** The ordered method is context → location → confirmation, not one fixed entry algorithm (JR 33, post 2093335135789719861).

1. **R/D — Choose the relevant completed range.** Main NY box 06:00–09:00 America/New_York; freeze high/low and width, EQ, quarters, open/close and external projections. Manual alternatives:20:00–20:30, 00:00–00:30, 03:00–03:30, 09:30–10:00, 10:00–10:30, 12:00–12:30, 15:00–15:30. These are distinct parents, not interchangeable measurements. NY timezone is printed in settings, although some execution-platform axes/tickets use an unstated zone (TBR 4–7; JR 13, 63–67).
2. **R/D — Form the context first.** Overnight size/balance and already-purged liquidity, previous RTH value and price range, current open, relative participation, news and observed reaction determine expected path and ambition. A large range favors nearer/one-sided objectives; compressed conditions may expand. Being outside value while inside the prior price range is a valid continuation context in the raw record; outside both with high RVOL is a particular case (TBR 12–24; JR 33–49).
3. **R/D — Separate references.** Range EQ is arithmetic, range open is the actual open, and volume POC/EV midpoint are different. SessionStat, EVRange, P-zones, previous RTH levels, London/Asia pivots and delta shelves remain separately identified. The May 19/20 blue lines are not arithmetic parent EQ: e.g.28915.75/28834.25 imply 28875, not the blue 28865 (TBR 4–5, 32–35; JR 9–10, 16–18, 24–26).
4. **R/E — Judas outbound then reversal.** Contextual outbound move begins around 09:30 toward exhaustion; close as 09:40–09:50 arrives. The reversal is a response after a swept edge, often around±0.5 W, back through internals/opposite liquidity; exact±0.5 touch is not universal. The manual describes average 80–120-point opportunities, not a mandatory daily two-trade or fixed-profit rule (TBR 8–11). A January 28 ticket prints 14:53:46/15:27:05 without timezone; if UTC its entry is 09:53:46 ET, so a turn-window label is not sufficient evidence of an exact fill gate (JR 70–71).
5. **R/D — Extended single-direction case.** An already-extended overnight move calls for reduced risk/expectations, confirmed EQ/quarter entries in the permitted direction and usually the nearer edge; the manual's before 10:00 preference belongs here. No universal author width ratio defines extended (TBR 12–14, 24).
6. **R/D — Purged/compressed case.** Relevant overnight stops/liquidity have already been taken, with SMT/compression supporting expansion; 09:40–09:50 can be an add/continuation window after that context, not an obligatory fade. One prior-RTH extreme touched is not equivalent to all significant overnight liquidity purged (TBR 12–17).
7. **R/E — Internal rotation.** Trade confirmed EQ/quarter or selected expected-range reactions in an appropriate wide/in-value case, often both directions, reducing ambition in grind. September 2 includes two losing attempts; July 21 chooses to finish after 20 minutes, a personal decision, not a universal cutoff (TBR 24; JR 3, 40–42).
8. **R/D — Extension fork.** After expansion, the beyond-edge band is H+[1.33, 1.66]W or L−[1.66, 1.33]W. It may reverse, or only retrace then continue; judge rejection and the remaining draw. The manual drawing continues below−4 after a failed retracement. September 9's lower-band long occurs around 10:40; an all-branches 10:00 cutoff excludes it (TBR 20–21; JR 23–26).
9. **R/D — Preferred confirmation, not mandatory universal timeframe.** Favorite reversal signatures are 2/3/5-minute OBs or rejection blocks; later trades add absorption candles, BigTrades, footprint/taper, local shelves and profiles. A large aggressive print alone can mark trapped buyers rather than support (TBR 27–31, 35; JR 5, 8, 14, 32–33, 39, 48–50).
10. **R/E — OB geometry and execution alternatives.** C2 sweeps C1; C3 closes beyond C 2's opposite extreme. The OB is C 2's full range. Drawings show entry at C 2's boundary, a midpoint-risk alternative, a conservative opposite-boundary stop and a midpoint-retracement entry. The C3 confirmation close and the drawn boundary entry need not have the same price; an implementation must state its fill convention (TBR 27–28).
11. **R/E — Rejection block.** Use the rejecting wick rather than the full OB. The drawing distinguishes wick-midpoint and wick-end stops; p 29 erroneously captions both as midpoint. Preserve the shown example geometry and leave a generic conservative rule unresolved (TBR 29).
12. **R/D — Destination before action.** Aim for case-appropriate EQ, range edge, opposite liquidity, extension or expected-range boundary; trail/protect, take partials or manually exit when the expected reaction fails. January 9 exits at EQ rejection; another position has a raised stop and an unfilled target. Tickets do not establish the original stop once amended (TBR 24–29; JR 7, 30–33, 42, 51–52, 69).
13. **R/E — Order and risk choices.** Market orders for volatility, limits for slower conditions, and stop execution/copier settings are alternatives. The 2-contract, 0.15%, 20-tick stop/120-tick target/6 R ticket is a settings example, not a daily universal preset. Expansive conditions permit greater ambition/runners/adds; reduce risk in grind/extended cases (TBR 24–26).
14. **R — Recognize failure.** Three unsuccessful attempts at one reversal level, no rejection, strong bodies/volume continuing through the level, wrong direction or an expired opportunity call for exit/observation/reassessment. If continuing after failure, reduce allocation at least 50%; do not keep fading because the level exists (TBR 37).
15. **R/D — Other clocks and news.** Delayed news can produce the true low after 10:00 and before 10:30; a 10:30 higher low can continue into noon. AM 9:30–12/lunch 12–13/PM 13–16 can alternate consolidation and expansion. The colored CPI/NFP/FOMC-week table is a stylized expectation, not a release-date algorithm (TBR 18–24, 36).
16. **R — PD RTH Range+ exception.** Prior 09:30–16:00 extremes and M 15/H 1 imbalances are context/destinations; wait for current RTH direction. ETH consumption does not remove an objective in this explicitly RTH-only view; that does not nullify overnight-purge analysis elsewhere (TBR 32–35).
17. **R/D — SessionStat/EV/P-zone envelopes.** SessionStat uses chosen session/lookback, mean/median and extensions; 60-session settings and 9–12, 9:30–16, H 4 examples are distinct. Below 2-minute and 5-minute chart aggregation can alter its calculations; the source favors above 2–3 minutes unless using 15 m/1 h. It cannot cross midnight directly and can fail in chop/low volatility. Exact Min Avg/EV/P-zone engines are not disclosed (SS 3–12; JR 26, 44, 69).
18. **R/E — Exposed settings are constraints, not engine recovery.** P-zone 500 learning sessions, percentile scaling, alignment, delete-invalidated, multiple sessions/tickers and auto T 3/T 4 are printed; S 2 differs 09:50 on one display versus 10:00 in another. Three-day pivot settings show London 02:00–07:05 and Asia 18:00–00:05; these are reference windows, not the manual's formation boxes (JR 16–18, 62).
19. **R — Distinct flow filters.** BigTrades NQ thresholds 100 NY/75 London; another footprint view filters top 35% transactions. Absorption Zone+ shows body 0.6, volume 1.5×14-period average. Neither execution size nor candle pattern proves passive replenishment (TBR 35; JR 49–50).
20. **E — Descriptive statistics.** The 4537-day console reports 09:47:36 original mean, 09:51:05 extended mean and 86.46% reversal statistic; it is not a trade win rate or every individual projection's hit rate. A separate 3249-day 09–12 table reports 44.8% double break, 28.0% high only, 26.1% low only, 1.1% no break; for 1.2%+ widths, n 169, 17.8/35.5/38.5/8.3%. A different 09:30–10:30 below-VAL sample hasn 396 inside prior range, n 517 below PDL. Keep populations/windows separate (TBR 30, 38; JR 37, 49, 70).
