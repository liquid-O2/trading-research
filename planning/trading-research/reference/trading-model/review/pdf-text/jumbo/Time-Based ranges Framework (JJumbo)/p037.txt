Model and Set Ups Failure

Recognizing Failure Signals

Failed 6-9 Model Reversal
   No Rejection Signature: When price reaches the projection levels during the 9:40-
    9:50 window but shows no rejection candles or orderblocks
    Continuous Momentum: Price continues through projection levels with strong
   momentum and volume, showing no signs of exhaustion
    Extended Bodies: Instead of wicks at projection levels, candles show extended
    bodies in the direction of the trend
     Daily direction mis-interpretation

Time Window Violations
    Missing the 9:40-9:50 Window: When price reverses significantly before or after our
     critical time window
    Extended Moves: Price moves beyond expected extensions without respecting
    time-based pivots
    Premature Reversals: Reversal occurs well before or after reaching the expected
    projection levels (e.g 10am news)

Risk Management During Failures

Early Recognition and Exit
     3-Strike Rule: If price attempts to reverse 3 times at a level without follow-through,
    consider the setup failed
   Volume Divergence: When price reaches critical levels but without corresponding
    volume signature
    Tight Initial Stops: Using the aggressive stop placement mentioned in the entry
    models section to limit damage on failed setups
Adjusting After Recognition
     Exit and Observe: Once a setup is confirmed as failed, exit positions and observe for
    the remainder of the session
    Switch to Alternative Models: Consider using different setups like the Single
    Breakout Model or look for opportunities in PM session
    Reduce Position Size: If continuing to trade after a failed setup, reduce your risk
     allocation by at least 50%

Key Takeaways

   1. Failing Fast: Recognizing failures quickly preserves capital and mental energy
   2. Contextual Awareness: Understanding why a setup failed improves future decision-
    making
   3. Statistical Mindset: No setup works 100% of the time; respect the statistical nature
    of market edges
   4. Risk Management: Having pre-defined failure protocols prevents emotional
    decision-making
