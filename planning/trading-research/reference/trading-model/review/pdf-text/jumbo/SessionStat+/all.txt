Introduction

The SessionStat+ indicator is a powerful and versatile tool designed
to empower traders with precise insights into market price dynamics
across multiple timeframes and custom sessions. By leveraging
statistical analysis of historical price movements, SessionStat+
identifies key price levels, including expected highs, lows, and
maximium/minimum distributions, enabling traders to anticipate
market behavior with confidence.
Whether you're trading on 15-minute, hourly, daily, weekly, or
custom-defined sessions, SessionStat+ calculates critical metrics
using both simple and weighted averages, as well as medians.
These calculations are visualized through intuitive lines, boxes, and
projections, providing a clear roadmap of support, resistance, and
breakout zones. With customizable settings for data lookback
periods, timezones, and visual styles, SessionStat+ adapts to any
trading strategy, from scalping to swing trading.

Key features include:
    Multi-Timeframe Analysis: Analyze price levels across standard
    timeframes or up to four custom sessions.
     Statistical Projections: Forecast potential price targets with upper and
    lower expansion zones based on historical data.
   Minimum Average levels: Detects potential swings areas that fall short
   from highs and lows.
    Visual Clarity: Display levels with customizable lines, shaded boxes, and
    midpoint lines for enhanced chart readability.
    Flexible Configuration: Tailor session times, averaging methods, and
    visual preferences to suit your trading style.

Whether you're a day trader, swing trader, or market analyst,
SessionStat+ equips you with the data-driven edge needed to make
informed decisions in dynamic markets.

Functionality


    Flexible Period Analysis:
   The indicator allows users to select a time period for analysis: 15-
    minute, 1-hour, 4-hour, daily, weekly, or custom sessions.
    For custom sessions, users can define up to four distinct sessions
    with specific start and end times, each with customizable colors
    for visualization.





    Level Calculations:
    Average and Median Levels: It calculates the average and median
    of the Highs and Lows over the lookback period chosen.
   Minimum average Levels: It computes additional levels based
   on the minimum average of the session, which reflects price
   movements of shorter swings.
    Projections: Optional custom upper and lower projections are
    calculated by extending the average range (difference between
   upper and lower levels).

 Accuracy & Data Detection


The levels in the indicator may differ when switching to a timeframe
below 2 minutes by design, to gather additional accuracy in the
levels. Consider using the indicator above 2-3 minute time-frame
(unless using the 1 hour or 15 minute non-custom chosen session)

Bar-Based Calculations and Session Detection:
   The indicator uses bar_index and time-based conditions to
    detect the start and end of periods or sessions. On lower
    timeframes, there are more bars per session, which can lead to
   more frequent updates or slight variations in how session
    boundaries are detected due to timestamp granularity.
    For example, a 1-minute chart has 5 times more bars than a 5-
   minute chart, so the exact bar where a session starts or ends
   might differ slightly, affecting when the indicator initializes or
   updates session data.


Practical Implications:
    Visual Differences: On timeframes below 5 minutes, you might
    notice levels appearing at slightly different prices or extending to
    different bars due to the finer granularity of data and potential
    misalignment in session detection.
   Performance Considerations: Lower timeframes generate more
    bars, increasing computational load and potentially causing the
    indicator to process data differently, especially for custom
    sessions with frequent updates.

Custom Sessions time
limitations

The Custom sessions feature are designed to work in current day
time stamps only (Midnight to Midnight), when selecting cross-day
times (e.g. 11pm - 5am) the indicator will automatically stop
plotting.

Single-Day Timestamp Logic:
   The indicator sets session start and end times using the current
    day's date. For example, when you define a session like 9:00
   AM–12:00 PM, it assumes both times are within the same day
     (e.g., today). If you try to set a session that crosses midnight
     (e.g., 11:00 PM yesterday to 2:00 AM today), the indicator
    doesn't account for the day change because it uses the current
    day's date for all timestamps.


If you want to set a custom session starting at 6:00 PM and
ending at or near midnight (e.g., for the Asia session such as
8:00 PM to 11:59 PM), there are specific settings you need to use
due to how the indicator handles session timing and resets.





Daily Reset at Midnight (00:00):
   The indicator ties session timestamps to the current day’s date.
    At midnight (00:00 in the specified timezone, default
    America/New_York), the date changes, and the indicator treats
    this as a new day, resetting session data. If a session’s end time
     is set to 00:00 or beyond, it may be interpreted as the next day,
    causing the session to fail or not plot correctly.
   To avoid this, you should set the session end time to 11:59 PM
    (23:59), the last minute of the current day and set “Extension
   Type” to “Extend Until Session End”

Key Session Times



9:00 AM–12:00 PM: New York AM Session (High and Low)

What It Represents

    Session Time: 9:00 AM to 12:00 PM New York time.
   Purpose: This session captures the New York AM session, which
    represents the morning trading activity in the U.S. financial
    markets, particularly for Index Futures
   High and Low: The indicator calculates the highest price (high)
   and lowest price (low) during this 3-hour window (9:00 AM–
    12:00 PM) on the current day. These levels are significant as
    they reflect the price range during the initial hours of active
    trading in New York, often a period of high volatility and
     liquidity.


9:30 AM–4:00 PM: Regular Trading Hours (RTH) Session (High
and Low of Day)

What It Represents

    Session Time: 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM New York time.
   Purpose: This session corresponds to the Regular Trading
   Hours (RTH) for major U.S. stock exchanges, such as the NYSE
   and NASDAQ, when most trading volume occurs. It captures the
    high and low of the trading day, representing the full range of
    price action during official market hours.
   High and Low: The indicator calculates the highest price (high)
   and lowest price (low) from 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM, effectively
    covering the entire day’s primary trading activity. These are
    considered the daily high and low because RTH encompasses
    the most significant trading period.

Levels Failure Potential


When and Why Levels Might Fail

Levels generated by the indicator may fail to act as reliable
support/resistance or be breached unpredictably in the following
scenarios:

Choppy Market Conditions:
   Why It Happens:
        In choppy markets, prices oscillate rapidly within a narrow range,
        often triggered by lack of directional momentum.
      The indicator’s levels are based on average historical ranges, which
       expect a certain degree of directional movement. Choppy
        conditions produce erratic, non-directional price action, which may
        misalign with these averages.
   Impact on Levels:
       High/Low Levels: The projected high/low may be repeatedly tested
      and breached in both directions, failing to hold as
        support/resistance.
        Projections: Extended projections may be too far from the choppy
        price action, rendering them irrelevant.
       Boxes: Boxes between average and median levels may be too wide
        or narrow, as choppy prices don’t respect historical ranges.

Low Volatility Environments:
   Why It Happens:
        In low-volatility periods, price ranges are much smaller than
         historical averages due to reduced trading activity or market
         interest. For instance, during a holiday or weekend, trading volume
      may drop, leading to tight price ranges.
      The indicator’s levels are based on past sessions with normal
          volatility, which may overestimate the expected high/low in a low-
         volatility market.
   Impact on Levels:
       High/Low Levels: Projected levels are too far from the actual price
        action. For example, if the historical average expansion is 10 points,
       but today’s range is only 2 points, the high level at open + 10 is
       unreachable.
        Projections: Projections are even further out, making them
         irrelevant.
       Boxes: Boxes may appear overly wide, as the actual price range is
      much smaller than the historical average range.
