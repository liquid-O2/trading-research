Levels Failure Potential


When and Why Levels Might Fail

Levels generated by the indicator may fail to act as reliable
support/resistance or be breached unpredictably in the following
scenarios:

Choppy Market Conditions:
   Why It Happens:
        In choppy markets, prices oscillate rapidly within a narrow range,
        often triggered by lack of directional momentum.
      The indicator’s levels are based on average historical ranges, which
       expect a certain degree of directional movement. Choppy
        conditions produce erratic, non-directional price action, which may
        misalign with these averages.
   Impact on Levels:
       High/Low Levels: The projected high/low may be repeatedly tested
      and breached in both directions, failing to hold as
        support/resistance.
        Projections: Extended projections may be too far from the choppy
        price action, rendering them irrelevant.
       Boxes: Boxes between average and median levels may be too wide
        or narrow, as choppy prices don’t respect historical ranges.

Low Volatility Environments:
   Why It Happens:
        In low-volatility periods, price ranges are much smaller than
         historical averages due to reduced trading activity or market
         interest. For instance, during a holiday or weekend, trading volume
      may drop, leading to tight price ranges.
      The indicator’s levels are based on past sessions with normal
          volatility, which may overestimate the expected high/low in a low-
         volatility market.
   Impact on Levels:
       High/Low Levels: Projected levels are too far from the actual price
        action. For example, if the historical average expansion is 10 points,
       but today’s range is only 2 points, the high level at open + 10 is
       unreachable.
        Projections: Projections are even further out, making them
         irrelevant.
       Boxes: Boxes may appear overly wide, as the actual price range is
      much smaller than the historical average range.
