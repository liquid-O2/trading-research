ETHOS ORDER FLOW / RESEARCH                                                      ABSTRACT




IN ONE PAGE

What this paper shows

We measured one idea on 199 million ticks of Nasdaq futures data: price levels built by
large aggressive orders, and the memory of whether those levels were defended when
price returned. Three results follow.



   01 / THE RAW CONCEPT LOSES

   Fading every touch of every level loses −0.285R per trade after costs. Only 42% of touches hold. Anyone
    selling "just fade the level" is selling this line.



   02 / SELECTION FLIPS THE SIGN

  A small statistical model reads each touch's context: has this level held before, was it built by real size,
   where does it sit in the day, and it separates the touches that hold from the ones that break (AUC 0.63
   against a 0.51 placebo). Taking only the best-graded touches flips expectancy to +0.14R per trade, on
   data the model never saw.



   03 / THE EDGE IS THE REFILL

   The profit concentrates almost entirely in one execution style: resting passively inside the level, where
   the defenders reload. The same signals traded with market orders lose money.




We then attack our own result with placebos, reversed splits,
rotating folds and a 100-configuration audit, and we publish the
losing quarter along with the winning ones.
