  THE BLUEPRINT13
 The confluence fallacy

  Keani risks more on setups with more confluences, and he believes, like almost every
  beginner, that more confluences means a higher chance of winning. Nobody ever checks
   it. So let's check it.

  Take two trades. Trade A has three confluences and you risk $150. Trade B has four and
  you risk double. Both make complete sense technically. Is the extra risk on B justified?
  You'd need the hit rate of each, and Keani has neither number. His evidence was memory
  and old Discord posts. He was doubling his risk on a feeling that the fourth confluence
  matters.



  "You're comparing it to nothing, because you have no data backing
  that hypothesis up." 18:58


 Now the worked example from the board, the single most useful piece of math in this
  PDF. Say you collect 20 trades on three-confluence setups and find a 70% win rate at 1:5
  risk-reward. Now demand a fourth confluence. Plausibly the win rate drops to 65%,
  maybe 60%. How, if the setup got stricter? Because the fourth confluence costs you: time
  hunting for it, fewer trades taken, good setups going untraded. The stricter filter can
  genuinely produce a worse account. Or a better one. You cannot know which until
  you've collected the 20 trades, and Keani, months in, had collected zero. His dashboard
  adds a second cost: his account can't afford lower frequency, so for him specifically,
  stacking probably makes things worse.

  To be fair: sometimes the fourth confluence does raise the hit rate. The fallacy was never
  in adding confluences. It's in adding them unmeasured.
