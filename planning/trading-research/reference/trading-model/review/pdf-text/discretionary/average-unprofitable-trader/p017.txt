  THE AUDIT17
 The four questions

  Before the interview I wrote four questions on the board. They were the skeleton of
  everything I asked Keani, and they're the audit I'd hand any struggling trader. Answer
  them honestly, in writing, before you buy your next eval.


  01   Do you collect data? Not "do you think about your trades." Not "do you post a thesis in a Discord
        sometimes." Do you have, written down, a sample of your own trades tagged by feature, regime and
        outcome, large enough to answer a question with? Keani's honest answer was no, and if yours is too,
        every other problem you think you have is unverified. This question comes first because every later
         question depends on it.

  02   Do you understand how a prop firm works? Can you explain, right now, how your account's trailing
       drawdown moves, where both barriers sit today, and what that implies for your size? If you're risking a
         fixed dollar amount inside a product whose walls move daily, the answer is no, whatever you'd like it to
         be.

  03   What is your main issue? (It's probably not the main issue.) This is the trap question. Keani said
         psychology. Nearly everyone says psychology, because psychology is unfalsifiable and flattering: it
         implies the strategy is fine and only the pilot needs work. But what feels like a discipline problem is
         usually a data problem or a risk problem casting a shadow. You over-trade because nothing in your risk
        design absorbs it. You hesitate because you have no sample proving the setup deserves trust.
         Interrogate the diagnosis before you treat it.

  04   Have you optimised your entries? Deliberately last. Entries are where beginners start, and they're the
         least of it. Optimising an entry inside a strategy with no data, emotional sizing and an unread product is
         polishing the door handles on a house with no foundations. Once the first three questions have real
        answers, entry optimisation becomes a measurable A/B test like everything else.
