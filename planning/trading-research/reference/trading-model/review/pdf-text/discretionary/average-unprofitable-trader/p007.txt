  THE DIAGNOSIS07
 The red list: four mistakes

  Before I drew a single chart in that call, I wrote four things in red, straight out of Keani's
 own answers in the first five minutes. I'd write the same four for most struggling traders I
  meet.


  01   No journaling, no data collection. His words: "I don't journal at all." 02:51 His entire data operation was
        a daily thesis in a Discord, glanced back at a week later. A thesis on a server is an opinion with a
        timestamp, nowhere near data collection.

  02   Assumption. His best winner of the week was a short during a Trump speech: "I just assumed that we
        would keep going down." 11:33 It paid. He called it luck himself. A trade that wins on an assumption
        teaches you nothing, and worse, it pays you to keep assuming.

  03   Outdated books. Trading books ten, twenty years old, treated as current. Reading feels like work, but
         trading information is data, and old data ages. Two-decade-stale inputs are competing against inputs
        from last month.

  04   Comparison and strategy hopping. Months wondering how less intelligent traders were outperforming
        him, switching approach every time the answer hurt. Nobody abandons a strategy they can prove works.
        Hopping is what the absence of proof feels like from the inside.



  "The only reason you would strategy hop is because you don't have
  sufficient data to even show that your strategy is profitable." 06:36
