  THE BLUEPRINT15
 The product you actually trade

  I asked Keani if he understands how a prop firm works. "No, I don't think so." Most
  funded traders would have to give the same answer, and it costs them the payout. You
  think you're trading the market. You're not:



  "A prop firm is a synthetic product... You're not actually trading a real-
  life market. You're trading a barrier-based product." 14:04


 A live account sends real orders to the CME. A prop account is a demo with two barriers
  painted on it: a loss limit below you, a payout threshold above you. Touch one and you're
  out, touch the other and you get paid. That's the entire game. And the barriers move: an
  end-of-day trailing drawdown rises with your profit and buffer. The rules are dynamic, so
  risking a static dollar amount inside a product with moving walls is a category error. You
  manage risk to the barriers, because the barriers are the product.

  Once you see it as a product, the economics invert. You pay roughly $150 for an account
  that can pay out $2,000, so your real maximum loss is the fee. Professional profitability is
  alpha, and alpha takes maths, statistics, capital and equipment you don't have. Here you
  don't need it: "You don't need to have a positive expected-value strategy. You only need
  variance. Your variance has to be profitable." 36:20 About three weeks of technical
  profitability, repeated: five accounts, three hit the payout barrier for $2K each, two die for
  $150 each, net positive even if the strategy is marginal. The caveat is a big one: variance
  without process is gambling with extra steps, and none of this guarantees a payout for
  anyone, ever.
