  THE BLUEPRINT19
 The data-collection system

  "Is journaling the best way to collect data?" Keani asked. The system is smaller and more
  boring than people expect. Two kinds of data: quantitative, the numbers a dashboard
  spits out; and qualitative, the written record of what you saw and why you acted.
  Beginners need the qualitative side first, because it tells you what to count.

   It starts with the daily thesis: where are we in the higher-timeframe auction, and who
  should be in control? Holding a short at the lower boundary of the curve? Possibly not.
  Buyers step in at a discount. "Thesis is extremely important, to filter your trades. As a
   filter." 22:08

  Then entries, and one term worth learning: the feature set. Thesis is a feature, entry is a
  feature; deeper, absorption is one, trapped buyers and sellers another, aggression
  another. Each is testable. Collect 10 to 20 trades per feature and let the sample declare
  the winner: absorption against trapped traders, level one against level two, drop the
  loser. Write the regime down every time, fade or continuation, because features are
  regime-specific: absorption suits fading a trend; trapped sellers pile up at the bottom of
  continuation trends.



 "Why are you asking me for a subjective judgment? You need to
  actually look at the day for yourself." 25:18


  That was my answer when Keani asked whether his p-day and b-day rule was "good," and
  I used to ask the same of better traders. Whatever anyone tells you is their judgment on
  their trades. Collect 20 to 30 of your own; if the rule made no difference, it's noise, drop
   it.
