What an option actually is


An option is a contract. It gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a set price (the strike)
by a set date (the expiration). That one idea, the right without the obligation, is what makes the whole market behave
the way it does.



   THE APPLE FARMER

    It is winter. Apples are coming in summer, so the harvest equipment will be in higher demand and cost more, say 300
   now and 800 later. So you pay the seller 100 today, a premium, to reserve today's price for the summer. If it is worth
   more later, you buy at the reserved price and you are up. If not, you walk away. Your only loss is the premium, and the
    seller keeps it either way. That is an option.


One language note that trips up orderflow traders: in options, a long is a call and a short is a put. Same idea as
futures, just wrapped in a contract. Every one of those contracts has to be hedged by a dealer, and that hedging is
what reaches across into NQ and ES.



   CALL = LONG                                    PUT = SHORT

   The right to buy at the strike. You profit if price rises.          The right to sell at the strike. You profit if price falls.
