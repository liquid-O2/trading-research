11 Trading as a business



  the analogy that made data collection non negotiable


  The line that reframed everything for him is the same one used throughout the mentorship:

  trading is a business, and every trade is capital you're putting into it. If you were handing money

  to an actual business and it couldn't show you its expected value, its return on capital, any stats

  at all, you wouldn't invest. An index fund can tell you its average return is 8 to 10 percent with a

  roughly one in fifteen year down year. A trader putting real money into evals, into his own

  account, into his own trading, usually can't tell you any of that about himself.

     "Before you put any money in to invest in yourself into trading, into
    buying problems, into doing that stuff, you need to have data backing it.
    Otherwise there's no point."


  He says this is what changed his own relationship to a losing trade. Once he had a real sample

  behind his levels, a loss stopped being a reason to panic and became a data point his own stats

  already expected. That's the shift he keeps coming back to on the call: not more confidence

  from hoping harder, confidence built on a number he can actually point to.
