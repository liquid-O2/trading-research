07Step two: reward vs result

   If there's so much result coming in from aggression but price is just stalling, why would
  you enter? There's no reward. You want to enter on a reward system: your side acts, and
  the market pays them for it. Buy aggression followed by upticks. Sell aggression followed
  by downticks that stick. Effort alone is not the signal. Effort is what the losing side
  produces on the way to being absorbed.

  The research paper behind this series measured how early "at the touch" entries really
  are. Across every winning trade in the out-of-sample record, the median winner first
  dipped 18 ticks past the touch before it worked. The defense doesn't happen at the front
  edge of a level, it happens inside it. And the paper's cleanest live example is exactly the
  pattern this lesson teaches: the level gets tested twice, sellers get nothing both times,
  and only after the second test do buyers take control. Entering on the first touch is, on the
  numbers, usually just early. Re-entering after it fails is paying twice for being early.


  Effort is what the losing side shows. Reward is what the winning
  side gets. Enter on reward.




    THE EXECUTION VERSION OF THE SAME MISTAKE

     In the same paper, identical signals chased with market orders at the touch won 27.2% of the time and
      lost money, profit factor 0.81. Resting inside the level won 68.8% with a profit factor of 1.80. Same read,
    same engine, same costs. The only difference was whether you paid for the defense or got paid to be part
      of it. Caveat as always: historical simulation, modelled fills.
