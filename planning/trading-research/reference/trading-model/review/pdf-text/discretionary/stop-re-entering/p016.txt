16What this does not give you

  The three-step plan is an entry filter, and a filter is only as good as what flows into it.
  Without the higher-timeframe thesis, step one is a guess and the other two are filtering
  noise. In the bad clip that is exactly what happened: the location was wrong, so nothing
  downstream could save it. The thesis work is the prerequisite, not an optional extra, and
   it's the first thing taught in the trial for that reason.

 On the numbers: the 27% absorption-failure figure and the digit reads are from my own
  data collection and teaching, not peer-reviewed studies. The paper statistics are
  historical simulation after modelled costs, with a losing quarter sitting in the record,
  published. The behavioral studies below are real and heavily cited, but they describe
  populations, not you specifically. Nothing here is financial advice, and no number
  promises a future result.

  One last thing, because it's the honest shape of everything in this document: absorption
   is not a yes or no switch, it's a process with resiliency that changes second to second.
  Market-microstructure research on the limit order book treats that resiliency, how fast a
  level's depth actually refills after getting hit, as a real, measurable, and variable property
  of the market, not a constant. That's the academic version of what the four stages on
  page 10 are teaching you to read in real time. The checklist doesn't remove the
  uncertainty. It just tells you where in the process you actually are.
