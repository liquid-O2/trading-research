10Failed vs confirmed absorption

  Here is the uncomfortable part: failed absorption and confirmed absorption look identical
  at the moment most people enter. The difference only exists in time, which is why the
  checklist has stages instead of a snapshot. From the DOM lesson, absorption confirms in
  four:

  01     Initial defense. Aggression hits the level, volume runs above average, price holds. This is where
        beginners enter, and it is not confirmed. It can be temporary liquidity that gets pulled and stops you
          instantly.

  02   Replenishment. The level refreshes as it keeps getting hit, with maintained or growing size. Each refresh
           is the absorber telling you they're intentional. My minimum filter is three ticks of replenishment; one or
       two is the classic fake-out zone.

  03    Exhaustion. The aggressor's volume starts declining and delta turns against them. They're running out
         of conviction against a wall that won't move.

  04     Lift-off. The absorber flips from passive to aggressive and price accelerates away. That move is the
         reward, and it's the confirmation the first three stages were real.



    THE NUMBER THAT JUSTIFIES THE PATIENCE

    From my own data collection: absorption fails roughly 27% of the time when there's no pacing
     confirmation behind it. That's the gap between stage one and stage four, and it's where the re-entries live.
     Wait for replenishment and the failure rate drops sharply. This is a stated figure from my own records, not
     an academic study, and I treat it as context rather than gospel.
