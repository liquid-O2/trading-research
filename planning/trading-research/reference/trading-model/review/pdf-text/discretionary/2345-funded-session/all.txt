04 Reading an all time high environment



  sellers failing to reclaim control, and why the objective was neutral


  Going into the session, price had already made a strong move higher, and the read started with

  what sellers had failed to do: repeated attempts to reclaim the prior range, and repeated failure.

  The weekly delta profile showed heavy buying aggression behind the move, which is a different

  statement than "price is going up." It's a statement about who is actually in control of the

  auction, not just which way the last few candles printed.





  The grind higher this session's thesis was built against. Multiple failed attempts to reclaim the range
  below, each one adding to the case that buyers were priced in, not just leading.


  The objective going in was deliberately neutral. In a genuine all time high environment the

  correct approach is to wait for the pullback and trade within the directional push, not to predict

  a target in advance. Marrying a bias before the auction has given you structure is exactly the

  psychological trap this thesis process is built to avoid, reacting to where buyers and sellers

  should have control rather than guessing where price will land.

05 The entry signal



  a 350 percent divergence box, and an origin of the move entry


  The actual trigger was a tool that flags a specific condition: a 350 percent divergence between

  buying and selling aggression alongside a small imbalance, printed automatically as a green box

  when it fires. It is not a signal on its own, it is a flag that something worth looking at just

  happened, passive absorption on one side and aggressive pressure on the other, at the same

   price.





  The setup the box flagged: sellers exhausted, buyers priced in, entered as an origin of the move long once
   sellers who had been rewarded at this level earlier failed to hold it a second time.


  The higher timeframe context mattered as much as the local signal. Zooming out, this level was

  where sellers had previously been trapped on the way up, visible in the delta profile as buying

  aggression getting covered rather than reversed. That combination, a local divergence signal

   sitting inside a higher timeframe area where the opposing side was already known to be weak,

   is what separated this from a random entry on a green box alone.

06 Reading real absorption, not just a level
 that held before



  what the CVD was actually doing under an entire session


  A beginner reason for drawing a level is "price reacted here before." That's where most thesis

  building stops, and it's also where it should really start. The example used here is a level from a

  separate session, the one behind an earlier $18,000 payout request: a resistance area that had

  been used, and dumped from, multiple times.

     "Price rising = high conviction that session sellers had insane control."


  Looking only at price, that resistance area looks like normal supply. Looking at the CVD

  underneath it tells a different story: across the entire consolidation session, CVD was dying

  while price held or drifted up, meaning the passive limits at that level were being consumed and

  reloaded, not defended for free. That combination, price refusing to drop while the delta record

  shows no real selling pressure behind the level, is what extreme absorption actually looks like,

  and it's the difference between "this level worked before" and "this level is currently being

  tested and passed."





  The level in question, with the read marked on it live: price grinding up against resistance is not weakness
   in the level, if the order flow underneath shows sellers being absorbed rather than actually selling.

07 Trade 1, live: the long



  protected low trailing and a higher timeframe target


  The first trade recorded live was a long, entered once price showed the strength to push

  through a short term microbalance. Management was mechanical from there: once the position

  moved favourably, the stop trailed to the most recent protected low rather than a fixed distance,

  so the trade could only give back a defined amount of what it had already earned.





  The long entry, R:R 5.59 planned against a stop below the microbalance low that formed the entry.


  Target was set at a higher timeframe level rather than a fixed tick count, the same discipline as

  every other level in this document: react to structure that already exists, don't invent a round

  number to aim at.

08 Trade 1: trailing to target



  recovering yesterday's loss, plus the 500 left on the table


  Going in, this trade needed to do two things: recover a loss carried from the prior session, and

  add the 500 dollars left on the table the day before, when he chose not to trade the PM session

09 Trade 2, live: the short



  a target capped by consistency rules, and the session's last trade


  The second live trade was a short, entered on a lower push after the same kind of microbalance

  read as the first trade, this time working in the opposite direction. The target here was

  deliberately smaller than the setup alone would have called for, capped by a funded account

  consistency rule: a prop firm payout requirement that no single day can represent too large a

  share of total profit, which means sizing the plan to the account's rules, not just to the chart.





  The short, same mechanical management as the first trade: trail to protected structure, no fixed target
  beyond what the account's own consistency rule allowed for the day.


  This trade closed the session. Once it hit, the account was up 1.3k for the day off these two

  trades, and the call there was simple: done for the day, not because the setups stopped,

  because the plan for the session was already complete.
