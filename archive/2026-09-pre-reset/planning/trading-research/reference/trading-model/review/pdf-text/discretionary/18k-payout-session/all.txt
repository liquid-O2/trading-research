04 The thesis before the first trade



  a dealing range, a weekly delta print, and the gamma flip





  The higher timeframe view drawn out before the open: the prior dealing range, three minor volume nodes
  marked as the levels that had already produced reactions, and the weekly delta print underneath price.


  Before a single trade went out, the read was built top down. Price had broken a prior balance,

  come back to rebalance it, and the weekly delta footprint showed sellers trapped at the lows

  and exhausted on the way back up, buyers covering into that low. That is not a chart pattern. It

   is a record of who lost the fight for that price and who is now defending the win.


  The objective was set lower, because of where the balance sat on the yearly composite to the

   right of price: a lot of built-up value above and comparatively little below, which is the kind of

  asymmetry that makes one direction the path of least resistance rather than a guess.




     SHORT GAMMA, STATED AT THE OPEN

     "We have this and we are currently in a short gamma, and know that short gamma environment."

     Chat pointed out one gamma model was reading positive. The honest answer given live: price

     was sitting almost exactly on the flip between the two, close enough that either read was

      defensible, and the trade plan did not change because of it.

05 Trade 1: the pre-file entry



  Paying for buffer, and losing the toll





  The order ticket at the open: sold 1 at a 2.00 R:R, a small white box marking the pre-file entry itself,
  stopped for -$100 before the level ever confirmed.


  The first trade of the day went out before there was anything to confirm it, on purpose. "No, I

  actually risked really less in my first two trades." The point of a pre-file entry is not to be right, it

   is to buy a small amount of buffer on the session cheaply, in case the real setup a few minutes

   later needed the room. This one did not work. Sellers never got absorbed the way the thesis

  wanted, and the stop paid its toll for -$100 across the book.


  Graded honestly afterward, on reviewing the session: a B plus at best, not a strong setup,

  entered anyway because the cost of being wrong on a pre-file entry is small and known in

06 Trade 2: still too early



  The same mistake, once more, on purpose





  A second short order live on the book, -$40 risked, stopped moments later. Buyers absorbed the sell
  aggression that was supposed to confirm the level.


  The second trade is the one that gets cut from most recap videos, because it repeats the first

  one's mistake rather than fixing it. Sellers pushed in again, looked aggressive on the tape, and

  got stopped out by buyers who simply would not give the level up. "I might have just got

  checked out." Small size again, -$40, a scratch rather than a real loss, but a loss all the same.


  Two trades in and the session is down two, both on the short side, both before the market had

  actually shown its hand. That is not hesitation and it is not stubbornness. It is what testing a

  level properly looks like when the level does not cooperate: you pay the small, known price

  twice, and you do not change the thesis because of it. The thesis was never about these two

  trades. It was about what buyers and sellers did next.

07 Trade 3: the origin of the move



  Part one, where the real entry actually sits





  Marked "OFM" on the chart itself: sellers absorbed at this exact level before, and the read is that whoever
   is in control here proves it by holding the retest.


  "This would be the origin of the moves set up right here. If you go below this and retest it." That

   is the whole model in one sentence: find the price where control changed hands the first time,

  wait for the market to come back and test whether the same side still holds it, and only enter

  once that retest actually happens with real selling participation behind it, not just a touch.


  The first two attempts at this exact level did not have that participation and were let go. The

  third attempt did: "Now we need to go below, we retest it with a decent amount of aggression,

  I'm in." Sellers refreshed at the level rather than thinning out, which is the tell that separates a

  level worth defending from one that is about to give way.

   A level does not become the trade because price touched it. It becomes
     the trade when the same side defends it a second time, with more
     conviction than the first.

08 Trade 3: protected highs



 How a stop trails a move it does not yet believe in





   Mid-trail: the stop order has moved to +$775 locked in, the resting target sits at $2,300. Neither figure is
   the trade's final result, both are the position being managed live.


  The starting reward against the risk on this entry was 0.69, meaning the initial target was

  smaller than the initial stop. Most traders skip a trade shaped like that outright, because it fails

  the arithmetic before it fails the read. It is worth taking anyway when the level behind it is

  strong, because the reward the entry started with is not the reward the trade ends with.


  Once price took out the first swing low with enough aggression to close below it, that high

  became what gets called a protected high in this framework: a point the stop can move to and

  leave alone, because giving it back would mean the read was wrong in the first place. "Once we

  go below this, it becomes again a protected high, in terms of micro mechanics I can trail

   further." Each new low that closed with real speed of tape behind it created the next one, and

  the stop walked down behind price one protected high at a time rather than by a fixed distance.


  This is trailing convexity: the risk on the trade shrinks every time the market proves the read

  correct again, so the position gets to keep running long after a fixed stop would have taken

   profit and moved on.

11 Refresh orders and consistency



 Why a level that keeps refilling is not the same level twice


  A resting order gets hit, and a moment later the same size is back at the same price. That

  reappearance is a refresh, and on its own it says only that somebody wants this price defended.

  What actually matters is what happens on the second and third refresh, because that is where

  the bluff usually shows itself.


  A level backed by real size refreshes at a roughly steady pace and a roughly steady size: hit,

  replaced, hit, replaced, without the replacement visibly shrinking. That steadiness, not any

  single print, is what a large resting participant actually looks like on the tape, because a genuine

  defender does not run out of appetite three prints in. A level that is really just one order rotating

  through a queue thins out fast: each refresh a little smaller than the last, the pacing between

  them stretching out, until it stops coming back at all. That thinning is what precedes the level

   finally giving way, which is exactly the tell trade three's entry was built on: not that sellers

  showed up once, but that they kept showing up with the same conviction on the retest.




     FROM THE SESSION

      "Sellers common, they're refreshing pretty strongly." And later, watching a different level fail the

     same test: "these buyers are failing, they're refilling again, the refill concept with selling pressure

     coming in with aggressiveness." Same mechanism, read on both sides of the market.



  This is also why a single absorption print is never enough to act on by itself. Absorption only

  says an aggressive order got stopped passively once. Consistency across several refreshes is

  what tells you whether that was a wall or a coincidence.

12 Delta protection and covering the book



 Two different mechanisms wearing the same sentence


  "Covering" gets used two different ways in this session, and they are not the same mechanism

  even though they sound alike.


  The first is options dealer hedging, the mechanical side of the gamma discussion at the open.

  A dealer who has sold options is typically short gamma: as price moves against their position,

  they have to buy into strength and sell into weakness just to stay flat, not because they have a

  view, because their risk desk requires it. That hedging flow tends to amplify whatever the

  market is already doing rather than calm it down, which is the actual reason a short gamma

  regime produces faster, wider moves than a positive gamma one. It is regulatory-grade risk

  management, not a read.


  The second is ordinary short covering, which is what the session actually points at when price

   is read live: "buyers are covering themselves, here are these lows." Any participant who sold

  and is now underwater buys back to close the position out. It shows up on the tape as passive

  buying at a level that has already been tested and failed to break, and it is the mechanism

  behind the term this framework calls a protected high: a level defended by real covering, not by

  one large resting order, which is why giving it back would mean the read itself was wrong rather

  than the stop being merely close.




     THE DISTINCTION THAT MATTERS

      Dealer hedging happens because a firm's risk model requires it, regardless of what anyone thinks

      the market will do next. Short covering happens because a trader lost the argument and is

      closing the position. Both show up as buying pressure. Only one of them tells you anything about

    who is actually winning the fight for the price.

14 Before the next session



  What this session actually checks for


  BEFORE THE OPEN


      The higher timeframe dealing range is marked, with the minor volume nodes that have already produced a
       reaction.


      The weekly delta print is checked for who got trapped and who is covering.


      The gamma regime is named, even when it sits near the flip and the answer is genuinely close.



  ON THE ENTRY


     A pre-file entry is sized small on purpose, as buffer, never as a conviction trade.


     A level is only traded once it has been tested and defended a second time, with refreshing size behind it.

  MANAGING THE TRADE


      The stop moves to the next protected high only after price closes below the prior one with real aggression.


      Covering by the losing side is read as confirmation, not confused with dealer hedging flow.


     A later trade is only sized up once an earlier one is no longer at risk.
