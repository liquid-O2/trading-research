 The tape doesn't lie: his numbers

  Keani would have told you his main problem was psychology. His dashboard said
  something colder. When we pulled his stats up live, three numbers did all the talking.


  $64              1.27             $741 vs $452

    EXPECTANCY PER TRADE              PROFIT FACTOR                    AVG LOSS VS AVG WIN




  "Does this seem good to you?" "No, no, it's horrible." 20:31


  That second line is Keani grading his own account, and to his credit he didn't flinch. Now
  read the numbers the right way, because most beginners read them backwards. The
  expectancy is positive, so the account grinds upward on average. But the average loss is
 64% bigger than the average win. His losers cost more than his winners pay. His cost
  per unit is higher than his revenue per unit, and the only thing keeping him above water is
  win rate. One bad week of variance and the structure folds, which is exactly what kept
  happening to his evals.

  Here's the part that should bother you: he didn't know any of these numbers until I
  showed him. He had been sizing trades, grading setups and blaming psychology for
  months without once knowing what a trade cost him on average. You cannot fix a cost
  problem you've never measured. The caveat: one trader, one eval account, a small
  sample; a mirror to check yourself in, nothing more.
