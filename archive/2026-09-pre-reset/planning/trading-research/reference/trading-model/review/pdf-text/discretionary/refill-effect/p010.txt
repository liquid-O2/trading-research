04Where the money actually lives

  This is the section that gives the paper its name. It began as a failure.

  Our first backtests used a tight scalp bracket and looked wonderful. Then we replayed
  the identical signals against real tick-by-tick data and found the result depended entirely
  on which side of the bracket was assumed to fill first inside each bar. Optimistic
  assumption: heavily profitable. Pessimistic: heavily losing. Honest middle: a coin flip. The
  "edge" was an artifact of fill assumptions, so we threw it away and started over at the tick
  level.

  The tick data then explained why, and the reason is the refill itself: around a genuine
  defended level, the median eventual winner first dips 18 ticks past the touch before it
  works. The defence does not happen at the front edge of the zone. It happens inside it,
  where the resting orders reload.

 The refill, one layer deeper: what happens in the order book





  Each bar is resting buy orders at one price. The wall gets hit, price dips inside the zone, and if the defenders are real, fresh
  orders replace the filled ones before the sellers can break through. The trade is to be one of those replacing orders.
