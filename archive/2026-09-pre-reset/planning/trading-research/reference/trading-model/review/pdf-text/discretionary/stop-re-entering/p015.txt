15Rules that survive you

  Remember the opening: your risk management should allow you to overtrade and survive
   it, because some day you will. That is not permission, it's engineering. Two findings
  anchor how I size that engineering, one from professionals, one from my own research.

  So the standing rules this lesson assumes: a daily stop at minus four R, honored
  mechanically. A re-entry allowed only when the checklist passes again from zero,
  location, reward, delta, as if the earlier trade never existed. And position risk small
  enough that a string of bad entries is an annoyance, not an event. If your sizing makes
  one bad afternoon existential, no entry framework can save you from it.

  Look back at the cover for a second. Five failed re-entries at minus one R each is minus
  five R, past the minus four R stop this page just set. That's not a mistake in the picture,
  that's the honest version of the lesson: a trader honoring the daily stop never sees re-
  entry five. The stop doesn't just cap the damage, it deletes the worst version of the day
  before it happens. The sixth trade, the confirmed one, only exists to walk in because the
  rule kept the account alive to take it.


 The checklist filters the trade. The daily stop filters the day. You
 need both.
