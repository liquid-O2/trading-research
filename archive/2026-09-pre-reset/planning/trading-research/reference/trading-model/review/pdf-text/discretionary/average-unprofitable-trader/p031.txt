 What this claims, and what it doesn't

  Every educational PDF should end by telling you what it can't do. This is a case study of
  one trader. Keani's $64 expectancy, 1.27 profit factor and $741 average loss are one
  account's numbers, and nothing about a sample of one generalises cleanly. The academic
  figures in the margins are mostly retail CFD and equity day-trader populations, the
  closest well-measured cousins to a prop-firm order-flow trader. They describe baselines.
  They don't predict you.

  This document sells no entry edge, because it contains none. The 27% absorption stat is
  what collection produces, from my sample, on my markets. Yours will differ; that's the
  whole argument for building yours. The industry numbers deserve their own honesty too:
  Topstep discloses that only 33.3% of traders who reached the funded level ever received
  a payout, while FTMO publishes payout totals in the tens of millions but no pass rate at
   all. Firms pay, and most individuals still don't collect. Both things are true at once.

  The two verdicts of the whole case: collect data, and treat the prop firm as a product.
  "You're trading blindly at that point, and not blindly as in psychological, but as in
  data." 41:38 Your real loss is the fee: "I'm paying 150 bucks to make potentially 2K, the
 maximum I'm losing is just the cost I paid up front." 42:12 The upside, with repeatable
  variance and nothing else promised, is technically open-ended. Between the two sits the
  journal, the 20-trade sample, the loop. Boring, and it's the only part that's real. The flow
  map, the checklists and the rest of the education library are free at kanji.org.uk.
