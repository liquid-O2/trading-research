  THE DIAGNOSIS09
 The psychology myth

  Beginners believe losing streaks and over-trading are psychology problems. I want to
  push back on that hard, because it's the most expensive belief in this whole case. "It's
  just so easy to blame one, right, which is psychology." 06:23 Psychology is vague, so it
  absorbs blame without ever producing a fix. Ask the obvious question first:



  "You don't data collect. So how do you even know what you're doing is
  correct in the first place?" 05:54


  Without data you cannot distinguish a psychology problem from a strategy problem from
  plain variance. Three losses in a row might be tilt. It might also be a 70% win-rate
  strategy doing exactly what a 70% win-rate strategy does. You cannot know which
  without a sample, and blaming your mind is the easiest way to avoid building one.

  So here's what you build instead. Picture a box. Inside it is everything your discretionary
  framework permits: say absorption, exhaustion, trapped buyers and sellers. Outside it
  sits everything else the market offers: trend lines, support and resistance, the regimes
  that don't suit you. Inside the box you can act. Outside it, you don't trade, full stop. That
  boundary is your discretionary barrier, and defining it is the first act of data collection,
  because you can only measure a strategy that has edges. Once the box exists, noise
  becomes visible: trading daily-VP shelves off the DOM and randomly switching to
  footprint imbalances mid-session is noise by definition. Before the box, it just felt like
   flexibility.

  The honest caveat: real psychological pressure exists, and data doesn't delete it. It tells
  you whether psychology is actually your problem this week. Most weeks, for most
  beginners, it isn't.
