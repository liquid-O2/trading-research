03 What the volume profile actually measures



 why the bell curve is shaped the way it is


  The volume profile is an indicator that measures volume within price, not volume over time the

  way a normal volume bar does. Plot it against a session where price played back and forth

  inside a range, and the shape that results is a bell curve, because the majority of the traded

  volume sits where price actually spent the most time.


  That one relationship, more time at a price meaning more volume at that price, is what every

  other concept in this document is built from. It's used to determine areas of fair value,

  acceptance, rejection, support and resistance, high volume nodes, low volume nodes, and POC.

04 The three components: VAH, POC, VAL



  value area high and low, and the point of control between them


  Value area high is the highest price within the value area, the band where roughly 68 percent of

  the session's volume traded. Value area low is the same idea at the bottom of that band, the

  lowest price still inside that 68 percent. Between the two sits POC, the single price where the

  most volume of anywhere in the session traded.


  These three levels are the raw material for everything that follows. Marked correctly, they tell

  you where the market has already agreed to do business, which is a very different, much more

  concrete question than where you think price might go next.



  VAH, POC AND VAL MARKED ON A REAL SESSION

05 POC as a magnet



 why price keeps returning to it, and how to read a target from it


  His description of POC is direct: it's a magnet within the balance. Price comes back to it

  regardless of which direction it approaches from. Once that's the working assumption, the read

  becomes about which further level price is likely to reach, not whether it returns to POC at all.


   If price fails to break above POC, retesting it again and again without holding above, the more

   likely outcome is a full trip back down to value area low. If instead price retests aggressively and

  then pushes through POC with real aggression, value area high becomes the more likely target.

  The behavior around POC, not a guess about direction, is what separates the two reads.

06 Continuation vs reversal



 why he trades one far more than the other


  A continuation, in his framing, is price breaking out of the range, coming back into it for a retest,

  and then continuing in the direction of the original break. A reversal is price moving up into the

  range and simply turning the other way once inside it.


  His own preference is stated plainly: he trades continuations, and rarely trades reversals,

  because continuations give him a higher win rate. That's a personal statistic, not a claim about

  which read is theoretically correct, and it's worth carrying into every shape covered from here

  on, since most of them are read the same way, for a break and a retest, not a turn.

07 The balanced profile



 when neither side controls, and why these levels are the strongest


  A balanced profile is where both buyers and sellers agree on a specific price area, establishing

   fair value with neither side in overall control. Price plays end to end inside the range, sellers

  more dominant near the top, buyers more dominant near the bottom, neither strong enough to

  break the range outright.


  His recommendation is specific: levels built from a balanced profile are the strongest and most

  easily identifiable in the market, and they're the ones he prefers to build a plan around. The bell

  curve shape is the visual signature, volume concentrated in the middle, tapering evenly at both

  extremes.



  THE BALANCED PROFILE: PRICE PLAYING END TO END, NEITHER SIDE IN CONTROL

08 The double distribution



  his favorite structure for building context, with a full trade example


  A double distribution is where both sides initially agree on a price and establish fair value, then

  one side takes control and drives price away into a fresh imbalance, before both sides come

  back into agreement at a new price and build a second area of fair value. The result is two

  distinct shelves on the profile, one above, one below, joined by the leg that connects them.


  This is one of his favorite structures for building context specifically because it gives you a plan

  before price ever gets there. With two shelves marked, if price opens and comes down to retest

  the lower shelf and buyers hold it, the read is a long back toward the upper extreme, entry at

  the retest, target at the far shelf. If price opens lower instead and sellers break through with a

  retest that holds, the same logic runs in reverse: short the retest, target the next extreme down.



  TWO SHELVES, ONE LEG: THE RETEST MARKED, ENTRY AND TARGET SET IN ADVANCE

09 The trending profile



 when to do nothing, and the mistake that blows accounts


  A trending profile is what happens when one side maintains control for the entire session,

  continuously driving price in one direction while the other side never gains enough footing to

  establish a balanced area of fair value. The result is a directional auction with little acceptance

  anywhere along the way, and on the profile itself, a shape with no real bell curve, just a long,

  narrow spread of volume.


  His instruction for this shape is the whole point of including it: when you see one, get off.

  There's nothing to trade. He names the specific mistake beginners make against it directly:

  seeing a strong trend and repeatedly trying to long a small reaction inside it, again and again,

  taken out each time, until the account is gone. A trending profile isn't a setup, it's a signal to

  stop looking for one.



  THE TRENDING PROFILE: ONE SIDE IN CONTROL ALL SESSION, NO FAIR VALUE ESTABLISHED

10 The P-shaped profile



  one side drives, then both agree, at the top


  A P-shaped profile forms when one side, typically buyers, initially drives price higher, and is

  then followed by both sides finding agreement at that higher price, building a new area of fair

  value there. On the profile itself this leaves a thin stem below and a wide bulge of volume at the

  top, the letter it's named for.


  Once that upper fair value is established, the read is the same continuation logic from earlier in

  the document: don't chase the trend that built the shape, wait for the range at the top to

  resolve. If buyers are dominant inside it and price breaks out with a retest that holds, that's the

  long, continuing the move the shape was already built on.



  THE P-SHAPED PROFILE: A DRIVE HIGHER, THEN AGREEMENT AT THE TOP

11 The B-shaped profile



  the same idea, mirrored to the downside


  A B-shaped profile is the exact same mechanism as the P-shape, run in the other direction: one

  side, this time sellers, drives price lower first, and both sides then come into agreement at that

  lower price, building fair value there. The profile shows a wide bulge of volume at the bottom

  and a thin stem above it.


  Read it the same way as the P-shape, mirrored: don't fight the drive that built it, read the range

  once fair value is established, and with a B-shaped profile the underlying trend is lower, the

  same way a P-shape says the underlying trend is higher.



  THE B-SHAPED PROFILE: THE MIRROR OF THE P-SHAPE, TREND READ AS LOWER

12 The checklist



  every rule in this document, printable


  THE COMPONENTS


        I've marked VAH, POC and VAL before forming any opinion about direction.


      I'm reading POC behavior (repeated failure vs. an aggressive break) to decide between the VAL and VAH

       targets.



  CONTINUATION OVER REVERSAL


        I have a break AND a retest that held, not just a level being touched.



  READING THE SHAPE BEFORE THE SESSION


        I know whether I'm looking at a balanced, double distribution, trending, P-shaped or B-shaped profile.


         If it's trending, I've accepted there's nothing to trade rather than fighting it.


         If it's a double distribution, I have both shelves marked and a plan for either direction before price gets
       there.
