Risk, the non-negotiables





The foundations, recorded with orderxfilled. None of this is optional.



       Predefine the stop before entry
       Hope is not a strategy. The stop exists before the trade does, and it never moves to avoid pain. Losses are
          tuition, pay and move on.





      Never revenge trade
         Losing a trade is not a reason to risk more. That sentence has saved more accounts than any setup ever
           will.





      Break even, but not too early
        Moving the stop to entry turns potential losses into neutral outcomes. Do it too early and you cut every
        winner short, do it at the logical spot.





      Cut losers fast
       Once the idea is invalid, exit immediately. Sitting in a dead trade is paying rent on a condemned building.





       Risk to the account, not the ego
         Overleveraging destroys accounts faster than bad setups. 0.5 to 2% per trade, consistently. You cannot
         control the market, you can control the exposure.

Exits, static and dynamic





Exits need logic and structure, not emotion. Two families, and the account type decides the mix.





   STATIC EXITS

    Fixed risk to reward ratios, predefined targets like VWAP, single get-in get-out setups. Decided before entry, executed
    without debate.





   DYNAMIC EXITS

    Trailing stops, partial scaling, volatility based exits, time based exits. The trade earns its room as it proves itself.





   FUNDED ACCOUNTS        Favour fixed RR plus one partial. The drawdown rules punish creativity, structure
                                 wins.





   PERSONAL ACCOUNTS        Trail stops, leave runners, use break even management. Your account, your
                                   convexity.





   THE GOAL

    Protect profits without strangling potential. Every exit rule is a trade-off between those two, pick the trade-off
   on purpose.

The five traps





Success is edge plus risk management plus psychology. The five traps that eat the third pillar:





  FOMO                      Missing trades costs nothing. Forcing trades costs everything.





   LOSS AVERSION                 If fear is driving decisions, reduce size until the fear disappears. Then earn it back.





   OVERTRADING               Usually boredom or revenge in disguise. The fix is a written definition of the A+
                                 setup, and permission to do nothing until it appears.





   EUPHORIA                    After the big win, pride books the next loss. The best session to go flat is the one
                                     right after your best session.





   ATTACHMENT            Do not tie your identity to one setup or one idea. The aim is to trade well, not to be
                                        right.

Bias, balances and the auction





Context is what separates order flow traders from button clickers. Know your objectives and where you are on the
map.




Price can only do three things: stay in a balance, leave a balance, or
return to a previous one.





   THE OBJECTIVES            Single prints from the TPO chart and balances, the zones of fair value. These are
                               the destinations price trades between.





   INSIDE A BALANCE              If your level sits inside a balance, avoid trading it. Choppy, low edge, the auction
                             has not decided anything there.





   AT THE EXTREMES          Trade levels at the edges of balance or outside it, where decisions actually
                              happen.





   THE EXAMPLE LOGIC

    Rejection from a balance with an unfilled single print above: bullish bias. Acceptance below a balance: bearish
    bias. Higher timeframe narrative first, lower timeframe entries second.

Flexibility and the weekly loop


The last piece is staying flexible without becoming random.

       Treat the thesis like a box
       The bias has a validity zone. While price respects the box, the bias lives. The moment price violates it, build a
      new bias, do not defend the old one.


       Build the weekly bias every Sunday
       Then compare it against professional traders, Jim Dalton, Rizzo, and study the differences. The gap between
        your read and theirs is the lesson.


       Journal the context, not just the trades
         Biases, context, emotional state. The journal is where the loop closes.





   THE INTRADAY TWEAK

    Set the value area at 40% instead of 70% for intraday work. The tighter range produces cleaner, more frequent reactions
    at the edges.





   THE SUMMARY

   There is no magic pattern. Discipline, structure and adaptability: master risk, strengthen psychology, define exits, and
   use the auction to trade with narrative and purpose.
