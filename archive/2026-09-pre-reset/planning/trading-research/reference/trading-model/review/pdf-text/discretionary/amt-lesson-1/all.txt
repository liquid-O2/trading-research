What AMT actually is





First things first: auction market theory is not a strategy. It is a framework, a way of reading what the market is
actually doing at any moment. You do not trade AMT on its own. You use it to know where you are on the map, then
you let the entry triggers do the entering.



The market only ever does two things: balance and imbalance.





   BALANCE, FAIR VALUE                               IMBALANCE, INEFFICIENCY

    builds around the middle, and the profile prints a bell            the next area of agreement. Thin volume, fast moves,

AMT can be drawn two ways, TPO graphs and volume profile graphs. Those get their own lessons. Everything in this
one uses the bell curves from volume profile, because they are the fastest way to see the auction.

  WHY IT MATTERS

   Every tool in the method sits on top of this. If you cannot say whether the market is balancing or imbalancing
    right now, no entry trigger can save you, because you do not know which kind of trade the market is offering.
   The whole lesson is those three questions, asked in order.

Balance and imbalance


Price always moves to find balance. When it moves out of balance it is in imbalance, and it will do one of two things:
return to the old balance, or create a new one somewhere else. That is the whole engine.





Roughly 70% of the data in the bell curve is the balance. The fat part of the profile is where the market agreed.

The practical use: treat balance and imbalance areas as levels to play off with the DOM or the footprint. They are your
dynamic support and resistance. The area tells you where the trade lives, the tape tells you when to take it.





Balance and imbalance marked on NQ. Price dips out of balance, uses it as support, then pushes to seek the next balance.
The note on the right is the 80% rule at work.

The value area: POC, VAH, VAL


Inside every balance there are three prices that matter more than the rest. They are the extremes of the value area, and
they are where the auction makes its decisions.

POC               Point of Control. The price where the most trading happened, the busiest shelf in the profile. The auction
                    gravitates back to it.

VAH              Value Area High. The top boundary of the zone holding roughly 70% of the volume. The ceiling of agreement.

VAL              Value Area Low. The bottom boundary of that same 70% zone. The floor of agreement.





POC, VAH and VAL on the profile. Busiest price, top of value, bottom of value.

The shape of the profile





The profile is not just levels, its shape is a sentence. Four letters cover most of what you will see, and each one tells
you who has been doing the business and what usually comes next.





      D             P             b            B

         BALANCED                  SHORT COVERING               LONG LIQUIDATION             DOUBLE DISTRIBUTION





  A two sided auction. Fat           Fat top, thin tail below.            Fat bottom, thin tail above.       Two separate balances
   middle, symmetric. The           Shorts getting squeezed          Longs puking, sellers              joined by a thin bridge.
  market agrees. Fade the            out, buyers finishing.                finishing. Common late in          Treat each as its own value
  edges back toward the         Common late in up moves.       down moves. Same                area, the bridge is the line
  POC.                           Expect balance after.              warning, reversed.                   in the sand.





  HOW TO USE THE SHAPES

   The thin part of any profile is the imbalance, business that never got done properly. Thin zones break fast and
    act as the reference when price comes back. The fat part is the magnet. Thin repels, fat attracts, that one line
   covers most profile reading.





   P AND B ARE WARNINGS, NOT SIGNALS

   A P shape late in a rally or a b shape late in a sell off tells you the move is being finished by the losing side
    covering, not by fresh conviction. It does not call the top or bottom, it tells you to stop chasing and start
   watching the balance that forms next.

The failed auction setup




This is the failed auction setup, the play the whole lesson builds to. When the market breaks out of value and later
trades back into a prior value area, around 80% of the time it traverses that value in the opposite direction.



Re-entry into old value is the highest odds rotation in the auction.
That is how you catch the fade of a trend.





          VAH


                                                                      80% odds it traverses to the other side
          POC




          VAL



                                                breakout fails, price re-enters value


The failed auction setup drawn out. Price breaks below value, fails to hold, re-enters at the VAL, and traverses the
whole area back to the VAH.




Price accepted back inside a prior balance has failed to hold the breakout. The auction treats the whole old value
area as unfinished business and rotates across it, usually toward the opposite extreme. The strict version traders
quote: once price re-enters and holds inside value, expect the full traverse.





   THE PLAY

   Mark prior value areas. When price breaks out and later re-enters one, look for the rotation across it toward the
   other side, and confirm the entry at the edge with the DOM or the footprint before you touch it.

Acceptance and failed auctions





A breakout on its own means nothing. What decides everything is whether the new prices get accepted.





   ACCEPTANCE

    Price breaks out of fair value on significant volume with convincing price action, and holds. The market participants
   agreed on new prices. The old boundary flips, support becomes resistance or the other way round, and you can expect
    continuation once the old value area gets retested.





   FAILED AUCTION

    Price pokes outside value and does not gain acceptance. No increase in volume on the breakout, long wicks, a quick
    return back through the point of breakout. These are the V shape reversals straight back to the levels.





For price action traders these are the same support and resistance flips you already know, just with the reason
underneath them. Volume confirms the flip, the wick exposes the failure.





  HOW TO TELL THEM APART

   Volume and behaviour at the edge. Acceptance builds volume outside value and holds the retest. A failed
   auction is quiet on the break, wicks hard, and snaps back inside within a few rotations.

The two rules of the auction


Two rules govern what happens next, and between them they cover most sessions you will ever trade.

       Rule one, balance stays balanced until proven otherwise
         Price inside a balance tends to remain inside it, and the extremes of that balance hold as support and
         resistance. Fade the edges back toward the POC until the market shows you acceptance outside.





Rule one on an order flow platform. The balance extremes hold, and the rotation trades edge to edge.

       Rule two, out of balance the ledges carry the move
      When price leaves a balance, the ledges of the prior balance are what hold for the move to continue. The level
         that was support becomes the resistance that fuels the next leg, and the reverse on the way up.





Rule two in action. Price leaves the balance and the prior ledge holds as the move continues.

Day types



Sessions are not all the same animal. AMT sorts them into day types, and knowing which one you are in by late
morning decides which layer of your edge is even allowed to trade.




   TREND DAY              Opens near one extreme, closes near the other. Value migrates all session, every
                                pullback is shallow and gets bought or sold again. The profile prints long and thin.
                                Continuation only, never fade it.





   NORMAL DAY           A wide early range, then rotation inside it for the rest of the session. The profile
                                  fattens into a D. Fade the extremes, target the POC.





   NORMAL VARIATION        The early range gets extended once, roughly doubling it, then the market balances
                                      in the new area. One directional push, then rotation. Trade the push, then switch
                                  to fading.





   NEUTRAL DAY             Range extension on both sides of the open, the auction genuinely undecided.
                               Close in the middle means confusion, close at an extreme tells you who won late.
                           Keep size small until it picks.





   NON TREND DAY          Nobody shows up. Narrow, quiet, usually in front of news. The edge is knowing
                                there is no edge, stand down or scalp tiny.





   THE POINT

   Most blown accounts are a day type error: running continuation on a normal day, or fading a trend day because
    price looks far from value. Name the day before you size the trade.

Reading the open




The first half hour tells you more about the coming session than any indicator, because the open is where the
overnight decision gets revealed. Four open types, in order of conviction.





   OPEN DRIVE                  Price drives hard in one direction straight off the open and never trades back
                               through it. The strongest statement the market can make. Do not fade it, look for
                                 continuation entries on shallow pullbacks.





   OPEN TEST DRIVE          The market opens, tests a key reference first, prior low, prior value edge, finds no
                               business there, then drives the other way. Second strongest, and it hands you the
                                    level your risk lives behind.





   OPEN REJECTION          Opens, auctions one way, gets rejected and trades back through the open.
   REVERSE                 Moderate conviction, expect two sided trade and treat the rejection extreme as
                               the day's reference.





   OPEN AUCTION               Price rotates around the open with no conviction either way. Nobody is in control,
                               expect a balance day and treat the early extremes as fade material.





  WHY THIS PAIRS WITH DAY TYPES

   The open type is the earliest read on the day type. An open drive points at a trend day. An open auction points
    at a normal day. You are building the same picture from two angles before most traders have taken their first
    loss.

The pre-open checklist



Six checks, in order, before every session. The first three happen before the open, the last three in the first half hour.
Run it the same way every day until it is boring, boring is the point.


      Mark yesterday's value
        POC, VAH, VAL and the balance ledges. Five minutes, done before the open, every session.



       Locate the overnight
         Is price inside or outside yesterday's value right now? Outside sets up the 80% rule, inside points at
          rotation.



        List the untouched references
        Old POCs never retested, prior session highs and lows, the ledges of old balances. These are the magnets
       and the targets.



      Read the open type
          First 30 minutes: drive, test drive, rejection reverse, or auction. That is your earliest day type call.



     Name the day type, pick the layer
        Balance day, fade the edges. Trend day, continuation only. Say it out loud before the first trade.



      Confirm at the level with the tape
       No DOM or footprint confirmation, no trade. AMT gives the where, never the when.





   THE WHOLE ROUTINE IS TEN MINUTES

   That is the entire cost of never trading blind again. Most traders spend more time scrolling for setups than it
   takes to map the auction that actually decides them.

Common mistakes



The ways traders lose money with AMT are predictable, which means they are avoidable. All five below are the same
root error: treating a framework like a signal.


       Trading AMT on its own
       The levels are context. Without a trigger from the tape you are just guessing at lines, the same as every
        support and resistance trader you have seen blow up.




       Fading a trend day
         Price being far from value is not a fade signal, on a trend day it is the definition of the day. Name the day
        type first, the fade layer is only allowed on balance days.




       Treating the 80% rule as a promise
           It is odds, not certainty. The one time in five it fails will hurt if you sized like it could not.




      Marking every level on the chart
        Too many levels is the same as no levels. Prior value areas, untested POCs and the old balance ledges, that
           is the list. If everything is a level, nothing is.




      Reading a finished profile on a live day
       The profile builds all session. A D shape at 11am can be a trend day by 2pm. Re-read after every big
         impulse, the same way you re-read gamma.





   THE FIX FOR ALL FIVE

   One habit: before every entry, say the day type and the level out loud, then ask what the tape shows at that
    level right now. If you cannot answer both in one sentence each, there is no trade yet.
