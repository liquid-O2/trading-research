  THE DIAGNOSIS11
 Risk that survives you


  "Your risk management should allow you to over-trade." 10:10


  Read that again, because every influencer tells you the opposite: become a robot who
  never takes a revenge trade. That's a fantasy. You're human, you will over-trade, and
  pretending otherwise means your risk plan assumes a person who doesn't exist. Design
  for the person who does.

  From my own data collection, I know it takes me two to three losses before I notice I've
  tipped into over-trading: "my risk management allows me to survive those two to three
  losses." 10:25 So my per-trade risk is sized so that two to three unplanned losses don't
  matter. The mistake gets absorbed by the structure before I've even caught it. That's the
  standard: risk sized for your worst documented behavior, and you only know your worst
  behavior if you've collected it.

 Now compare Keani's answer when I asked how he sizes: "I risk just based off of, like, in
  the moment how I'm feeling and what the account needs." 11:58 And the worse admission
  underneath: when he suspects he's revenge trading, he sizes up. Maximum size at the
 moment of minimum judgment. Emotions cannot be quantified, so they cannot be a
  sizing input, ever.

 On the board, "static $200" got the strikethrough. Beginners risk the same dollar figure
  on an A setup and a C setup, which makes no sense once you say it out loud. Best setups
  deserve more money, worst deserve less: size flexes with setup quality, while targets
  don't flex at all, staying where the auction logically pays. Keani had half the instinct
  already, grading by confluences. What he didn't have was any data proving his grades
  meant anything. That's the next chapter.
