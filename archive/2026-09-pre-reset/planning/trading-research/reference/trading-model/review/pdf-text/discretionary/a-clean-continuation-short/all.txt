03 Being honest about variance



 why this trade, and why not every week looks like this one


  This is a full trade breakdown of a continuation short: the pre-market thesis, the entry triggers,

  a stop-out, the re-entry, and two more setups from the same session. On the day, this account

  closed up $870 across two trades, close to a payout.

04 The 5 minute thesis



  a minor volume node, and negative Delta stacking against the level


  On the 5 minute, price had displaced lower and value had shifted down with it. Once price found

  balance, a minor volume node stood out as the level to use, a place used previously, multiple

  times, as resistance. In terms of Delta, negative Delta was stacking and stacking into that level,

  and cross-checked against the DOM and heat map, a clear pattern of sellers pinning the level.


  That combination, a real minor volume node plus visibly stacking negative Delta into it, was the

  read: be lenient on sellers here, expect buyers to be absorbed and exhausted, and treat this as

  a good area for price to come back into balance from. He also names this level a failed auction

  at the top, the wicks that never actually closed and held above it.



  THE 5 MINUTE THESIS: MINOR VOLUME NODE, NEGATIVE DELTA STACKING

05 The AMT structure



  an established balance, POC as fair value, and failed auctions at the top


  With an established balance in place and POC treated as the area of fair value, the framework is

  simple to state: if price comes back to the top of that balance, the default expectation is shorts,

  unless price closes above it with real, extreme buying pressure. If that pressure shows up, the

  read flips to a retest and longs back toward the session's VWAP instead.


  The areas marked here are failed auctions, in his words, more than once: price wicking above

  the level without ever truly closing and holding there. One of those wicks, worked on a lower

  timeframe like the 40 range or 1 minute, would have been good for roughly three to four R on its

  own, before price came back down.



  FAILED AUCTIONS AT THE TOP: WICKS THAT NEVER CLOSED AND HELD

06 Why the exit wasn't POC



  consistency rules, and reading a gamma long environment for chop risk


  The exit on this trade came off a transition in established value, not off POC, even though POC

  would also have worked as a valid take profit. The reason is deliberately not a pure order flow

  reason: consistency rules on a funded account, and the regime context for the week.


  For the prior week and a half, the market had mostly been sitting in a gamma long environment,

  which raises the odds of chop rather than a clean move all the way to the lower extreme. Taking

  the safer, closer exit reflected that risk rather than assuming this session would behave like a

  trend day. The two sessions immediately before this one had been in a negative gamma

  environment instead, which lines up with why price dropped and dumped into this balance in

  the first place.

07 The higher timeframe: yearly composite



  a low participation zone, sliced through, then used as resistance again


  Zooming out to the yearly composite, the level in play sat at an area of low participation, a zone

  price had previously sliced straight through rather than idled inside. Higher timeframe low

  volume nodes behave in one of two ways: price stalls and tries to build balance there, or, if

  there's enough selling pressure, it slices straight through. The Delta on that slice showed sellers

  clearly showing their hand, and price fully dumped through it.


  From there, price arrived at a yearly composite balance area, again with a nearby zone of low

  participation. The same previous balance from earlier in the session got touched again and used

  again as resistance to push price back down, slicing through the same low volume area a

  second time.



  THE YEARLY COMPOSITE: LOW PARTICIPATION, SLICED THROUGH TWICE

08 The refill zone, repeated



 why the first entry stopped out, and why the second one held


  The first attempt at this short got stopped out. The read going in was that buyers who had

  previously controlled the push higher would offer a passive refill zone for sellers, a place to

  drive price back down from. Buyers came back in instead, and the trade got stopped, which he's

  plain about: that's fine, it happens.


  What made the re-entry worth taking was that the same level kept behaving the same way on

  repeat. Sellers had control, price went lower. Price came back up to the level, sellers had

  control again. It went lower again. Sellers had had control there before, more than once, which

   is exactly what a refill zone is: a level where the same side keeps winning the argument. Buyers

  had controlled the push up, but the level itself kept refilling back to sellers, which is what the

  re-entry was built on.



  THE RE-ENTRY: SELL 1, R:R 6.11, TARGET $1,100 AGAINST A $180 STOP

09 Multi-timeframe alignment



  the dealing range VP lining POC up with the same refill zone


  Going back over the trade afterward, drawing out the volume profile for the actual dealing range

   in play showed the resistance used for the refill zone lined up with POC on that leg, on the 1

  minute. That's an intraday alignment on top of the higher timeframe read already covered: the

   refill zone and the dealing range's own fair value were the same price.


  He's clear that this alignment wasn't something checked in real time during the trade, it's

  something confirmed afterward, shown here because it's the kind of detail that makes a trade

   this clean actually explainable rather than a lucky guess. The setup overall: a balance rejection.

  Price came out of balance, used the level as resistance, pushed all the way back down, and that

  was a failed auction with buyers absorbed the whole way down.

10 The second trade: origin of the move,
 retaken



  a failed squeeze, a refill zone, then sellers show up again


  A second good trade from the same session came from retaking shorts at an origin of the move.

  Sellers showed their hand first, willing to price lower, which is what a genuine origin of the move

  entry needs: initiative, not just a guess. Taken there directly, it would have been a high risk

  entry, since the squeeze lower hadn't actually confirmed yet.


  Price came back up instead, into a refill zone where sellers had already shown control,

  effectively top-ticking there. From that point, sellers came back in with even more aggression

  than the first attempt, confirming that control was back with them despite the earlier failed

  squeeze. Price moved back toward intraday daily value, buyers hit the wall on the way and got

  absorbed, and that's the re-entry point, stop placed right there. Target: back to where sellers

  previously had no control, or more conservatively, the current intraday POC.



  THE ORIGIN OF THE MOVE MARKED (OFM), BIG TRADES SHOWING THE AGGRESSION BEHIND IT

11 The third setup: a squeeze with no failure



  the cleanest version of the same idea, no retry needed


  A third setup from the session is worth separating from the second: this one is actually a

  squeeze, not an origin of the move, because it never failed the way the second trade's first

  attempt did. Sellers showed willingness to price lower and the move happened fast, with real

  aggression, no retest, no false start.


  Once buyers hit that move and got absorbed, the entry is available with a tight stop right there,

  opposition visibly failing on both sides of the read at once. This is the same underlying model

  taught in the mentorship for catching continuation entries, whether that's framed as a squeeze,

  an origin of the move, or an earlier entry inside the same idea.

12 Why these were all the same trade



  filtering continuation entries against one higher timeframe bias


   All three setups in this document are continuation trades, and that's not a coincidence, it's the

   filter. The higher timeframe thesis was short from the open, so every entry taken had to agree

  with that bias before it was considered at all. A clean squeeze in the opposite direction would

  have been ignored, not because the read was wrong on its own terms, but because it disagreed

  with the higher timeframe context already established.


  That's the actual throughline of this whole session: one higher timeframe thesis, three separate

  lower timeframe confirmations that all pointed the same direction, at different points in the day.

  None of the three needed a new idea. Each one needed the same idea to show up again.

13 The checklist



  every rule in this document, printable


  THE THESIS


        I have a real minor or low volume node, not an arbitrary level, confirmed by Delta stacking against it.


        I know the higher timeframe balance and where POC sits as fair value before I look for an entry.



  MANAGING THE TRADE


     My exit reflects the actual gamma regime for the week, not an assumption that today is a trend day.


      I'm respecting my own consistency rules over squeezing every last point out of a winner.



  THE REFILL ZONE


        I can point to the same side winning control at this level more than once, not just once.


     A stop-out at this level doesn't disqualify a re-entry if the level's behavior hasn't actually changed.



  CONTINUATION ENTRIES


      Every entry I take agrees with my higher timeframe bias, not just the setup in isolation.


        I know whether this is a squeeze (no failure) or an origin of the move re-entry (after a failed squeeze), and
      I'm not treating them as identical.
